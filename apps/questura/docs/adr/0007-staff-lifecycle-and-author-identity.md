# Staff account lifecycle and author identity

Staff accounts gain a lifecycle state, and author identity moves out of the account. `Users` gets a **`status` field with values `active` and `disabled`**, and login and collection access are both gated on it. Deleting a row stops being the way a person leaves.

Today the only exit is delete, because roles are permanent by design — the role field permits `writer` → `editor` and nothing else, and admins cannot change their own — and no disabled or suspended state exists anywhere on the collection. So removing a compromised or departed editor means destroying the row, which takes the author slug, the public author page, and byline relationships with it. Disabling replaces that: a disabled account cannot authenticate and holds no access, while the row and everything referencing it survive. Roles also become **demotable** in both directions, so a person can be stepped down without being removed; admins still may not change their own role, which is the existing safeguard against locking the last admin out of role management.

Author identity moves to a new **`Authors`** collection holding `slug`, display name, bio, and social links, with a **nullable `user` relationship** to the staff account. Public pages at `/authors/<slug>` and article bylines read `Authors`, not `Users`. The relationship being nullable is the load-bearing part: an author record with no linked account is a valid, fully renderable state, so published work keeps its byline and its author page whether the person is disabled, deleted, or never had a staff account at all. Content stops depending on the continued existence of an employment record.

The slug stability rule carries over unchanged from the current `authorSlugHook` — a slug is generated from the display name when empty, is admin-only to edit, and changing it breaks inbound URLs — and now lives on `Authors`, where it belongs. Staff-facing consumers move to reading `status`: the staff list ABW renders and `isStaffEmail` treat `disabled` accounts as inactive rather than absent.

This requires a migration that creates `Authors`, backfills one record per `Users` row that currently has an `authorSlug`, links each to its account, and repoints byline relationships. That migration is the expensive part of this decision and the reason the smaller option — a `status` field alone, with author pages still reading `Users` — was considered. It was rejected because it leaves hard-delete destructive: disabling would be safe, but the delete button would still be there, still able to orphan a public URL and a byline. Removing the destructive path entirely is worth one migration.

Deleting a staff row remains possible, which matters for erasure requests, and after this change it is no longer catastrophic: the linked `Authors` record survives with a null `user`, so the byline and the public page are unaffected by the account's removal.

Alongside ADR-0006, this leaves `Users` with one job — authenticating humans who work on the CMS — and moves everything that outlives a person's tenure (their public authorship) or was never a person at all (machine callers) out of it. Note that ADR-0006, this ADR, and the pending removal of the dead staff-principal code all edit `Users.ts`; they are sequenced rather than parallel to avoid conflicting on the same file.
