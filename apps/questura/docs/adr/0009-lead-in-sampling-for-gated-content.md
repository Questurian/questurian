# Gated content is sold by lead-in sampling, and crawlers are not a caller class

Paid editorial content is gated by serving every unentitled caller a **Free sample** derived from a deterministic per-collection **Sample rule**, with the locked remainder absent from the response rather than concealed by the client. Search crawlers receive exactly the same Free sample as an anonymous reader. The page stays indexable through its title, meta, headings, sample text, and paywalled-content structured data (`isAccessibleForFree: false` plus `hasPart` with a `cssSelector`) — which is what keeps a short sample from reading as thin content, and what makes the paywall legible to Search rather than accidental.

Serving the full body to a verified Googlebot is the alternative, it is explicitly sanctioned by Google under flexible sampling, and it is what large publishers do. It was rejected on cost, not on legitimacy. It requires reverse-DNS verification of every claimed crawler, because a User-Agent string is one `curl` flag away from being free content for anyone who reads our source. It requires that the crawler-shaped response never enter the shared CDN cache, on a site whose public shell is `force-static` with hourly ISR precisely so that responses *are* shared. And it would have made the paywall's first week live also the crawler-verification code's first week live. The concrete price paid is real and should be named: phrases that appear only in the locked remainder are not indexed, so Gated items rank on their sample rather than their whole body. If organic performance on Gated items proves materially worse than on free ones, this is the decision to revisit, and revisiting it costs a reindex cycle in each direction.

Because there is one unauthenticated response for every caller, the existing cached public routes need no cache segmentation, no `Vary`, and no notion of who is asking — ADR-0003 survives untouched. The corollary is that a member's full body cannot be server-rendered: `(public)/layout.tsx` is `force-static`, where `cookies()` is empty by construction, so no page under the public shell can know who the reader is. The full body is therefore fetched by the browser from a dynamic, `no-store` route that requires a Current principal with an active Membership entitlement, and swapped into the locked region. Moving Gated pages to a dynamic shell instead would have bought flash-free rendering for members at the price of live Payload reads for every anonymous and crawler hit on exactly the pages most worth caching.

Enforcement lives where the body is serialized, not where it is queried, and this is the part most likely to be "fixed" wrongly later. Every public read calls `payload.find({ overrideAccess: true })`, so Payload collection access control is bypassed on all of them and cannot gate anything; tightening `Articles.access.read` would produce a comforting diff with no effect. The surface is small enough to hold in your head: exactly two public routes emit a full document, `articles/by-canonical-path` and `articles/by-id`, both through `serializeArticleByCollection`. Every other public route, and the curated homepage formatter, emits card-shaped data by construction. Internal search indexes the full body into its tsvector but returns only `{type, id, rank}` hydrated to card fields, so a Gated item can rank on locked text without ever displaying it — which is discovery working in our favour, not a leak.

The Sample rule is structural per collection rather than an editor-placed marker, because a marker has an unset state and a paywall with an unset state fails silently in both directions: a forgotten marker ships the item either entirely free or entirely locked, and nobody finds out from the code. Structural rules also land on each shape's natural seam — first blocks for a flat article, first entries for a ranked listicle, and Day 1 complete for an itinerary, which is both a clean cut and the best sales pitch available ("see Day 1 free, unlock all five days").

Verification is deliberately not required to read a Gated item, mirroring the existing deliberate decision not to require it for checkout. Requiring it on the read while checkout stays open would let a visitor complete a real charge and then be refused the content they just bought, which is the precise refund and chargeback exposure this work exists to close. `CONTEXT.md` previously claimed both were gated on verification; it was wrong about the shipped code, and was corrected rather than implemented.
