## Where to Stay Guide

### Purpose & Reader Intent

- **Purpose** – To help readers choose the right neighborhood and lodging for their travel style, budget, and needs. A strong guide explains how different areas feel, highlights tradeoffs of various accommodation types, and gives practical advice on logistics like proximity to attractions and transport.

- **Reader Intent** – Readers are decision-shopping, not reading linearly. They want to quickly match themselves to the right area and lodging type (families, nightlife, sightseeing, business, luxury, backpacking) and walk away with enough practical detail to book with confidence.


### Core Content Expectations (Organized by Reader Journey)

The guide should follow the reader's actual decision flow: **Where** should I stay → **What type** of place → **How** do I book it well → **What** should I watch out for.

#### 1. Where should I stay? (Orientation + neighborhood selection)

- **Destination overview and layout** – Begin with a concise introduction to how the city/region is laid out: which areas are central, which are suburban, how they connect, and what the overall "shape" of the place is. Readers need a mental map before they can evaluate neighborhoods.

- **Neighborhood-to-traveler mapping** – Explicitly match traveler types to neighborhoods using an "If you're X, stay in Y because Z" framework. This belongs near the top so skimmers get value immediately. Cover at minimum: families, couples, solo travelers (including solo women), backpackers/budget, luxury seekers, business travelers, LGBTQ+ travelers, and travelers with mobility needs.

- **Detailed neighborhood profiles** – For each recommended area, cover:
    - **Atmosphere** – What the area actually feels like at different times of day.
    - **Who it suits** – And importantly, who it *doesn't* suit.
    - **At least one tradeoff or caveat** – Every area has them; pretending otherwise erodes trust.
    - **Transport** – Specific stations/lines, not just "well-connected."
    - **Distances** – Expressed in time *and* mode (e.g., "12 min walk or 1 stop on Metro Line 1 to the Old Town"), not just kilometers.
    - **Safety** – Operationalized, not generic. Note specifics like well-lit main streets, known pickpocket hotspots, areas to avoid late at night, or "feels safe at all hours."
    - **One verifiable local detail** – A named landmark, specific street, café, market, or local quirk that a generic scrape of booking sites wouldn't produce. This is the single biggest anti-generic signal. (See example at end.)

#### 2. What type of place? (Accommodation categories)

- **Lodging categories** – Explain the categories available in this destination: chain hotels, boutique/independent hotels, guesthouses, apartments/short-term rentals, hostels, campgrounds, and any destination-specific types (ryokan, riad, pousada, etc.).

- **Tradeoffs, not just features** – For each category, state honest pros and cons. Chain hotels offer consistent standards but often lack local character. Apartments give space and kitchens but usually no front desk or daily service. Hostels are cheapest but vary wildly in quality.

- **Why this property in this place** – If named properties are mentioned, explain why *this* property in *this* location — not a generic list. Avoid writing that reads like a booking-site scrape.

#### 3. How do I book it well? (Practical booking guidance)

- **Concrete price tiers** – Include currency, per-night range, and what that tier typically includes in this specific destination. Example format:
    - Budget: $40–80/night — usually shared bathrooms, no breakfast, outside the tourist core
    - Midrange: $80–180/night — private bath, breakfast often included, central or near transit
    - Luxury: $250+/night — full service, prime location, amenities like spa/pool

- **Booking tactics** – How to read reviews critically (filter by recent, check the negative ones, watch for patterns), how to request specific rooms (higher floor, away from elevator, quiet side), how to confirm reservations directly with the property for peak dates.

- **Seasonal considerations** – Required unless the destination has no meaningful seasonal variation, in which case state that explicitly. Cover how prices and availability shift, when to book early, and which shoulder seasons offer best value.

#### 4. What should I watch out for? (Location criteria and red flags)

- **Location criteria** – Emphasize proximity to public transport, attractions, and dining; the central vs. suburban tradeoff; noise (main roads, nightlife strips, early-morning markets); and neighborhood-specific safety considerations.

- **Property-level red flags** – What to check in reviews and listings: misleading "near [landmark]" claims, photos that hide the building's actual surroundings, amenity fees, resort fees, extra charges for amenities that should be standard.


### Evidence Standard

Each neighborhood profile must include at least one concrete, verifiable detail that a generic AI-written or scraped guide wouldn't have — a specific street, a named café or market as a landmark, a local quirk (e.g., "the 7 a.m. bell from San Giorgio will wake you if you stay east of the canal"). Recommendations should be grounded in personal experience, local sources, or cited reporting — not vague impressions.


### Required Editorial Components

- **key_takeaways_box** – Mandatory. Place near the top. Use it as an at-a-glance traveler-type → neighborhood matchup so decision-shoppers get value without scrolling.

- **in_the_know_box** – Mandatory. This is the natural home for local tips and cultural context: tipping norms, check-in customs, unique lodging experiences (ryokan etiquette, riad layouts), local regulations (short-term rental rules, tourist taxes), or neighborhood quirks.

- **faq_block** – Mandatory. Short Q&A covering the most common traveler-type questions: "Best area for families?" "Best for nightlife?" "Where to stay without a car?" "Is X neighborhood safe at night?"

### Optional Editorial Components

- **highlight_callout** – Use for a standout property, a seasonal warning, or a specific booking window worth flagging. Don't force it — manufactured emphasis weakens the guide.

- **pull_quote** – Only use when there's a genuinely quotable line from a local, the writer's experience, or a traveler — not a fabricated or filler quote.


### What NOT to Do

- **No empty descriptors.** Avoid "vibrant atmosphere," "something for everyone," "charming streets," "hidden gem" without evidence. If you can't substantiate it with a specific detail, cut it.

- **No recommendation without tradeoff.** Every neighborhood and every lodging type has downsides. Name them.

- **No unsubstantiated property lists.** Don't list hotels or chains without explaining why *this* property in *this* location matters for the reader.

- **No vague safety language.** "Generally safe" tells readers nothing. Be specific about what, when, and where.

- **No distance-without-context.** "2 km from the center" is useless without the time and mode (walk, metro, taxi).

- **No neighborhood the writer can't substantiate.** If there's no personal experience, no local source, and no cited reporting behind a recommendation, it shouldn't be in the guide.


### Signs the Source Material Is Insufficient

- The source material lists accommodation names without describing neighborhoods or explaining why they're suitable.

- No information about location, amenities, service quality, or price ranges; the guide can't differentiate between lodging types.

- Lacks context on whom each area or lodging option suits (families vs. party-goers, solo travelers, accessibility needs).

- Omits practical booking advice or safety considerations.

- No verifiable local details — profiles read as if they could describe any city.

- No tradeoffs or caveats for any neighborhood or lodging type.


### Editorial Flexibility Notes

- Writers can prioritize neighborhoods differently based on the destination's character (e.g., leading with budget areas for backpacker-heavy destinations, upscale districts for luxury-focused content). Recommendations should still remain well-rounded across traveler types even when the emphasis shifts.

- The structure doesn't have to follow a strict list. Creative organization (e.g., grouping by themes like "historic districts," "beachfront areas," or "where locals actually live") is encouraged when it helps readers decide.

- Personal experience and anecdote are welcome and strengthen the guide — as long as they support rather than replace the core coverage.


### Example: Neighborhood Profile — Weak vs. Strong

**Weak (generic, unusable):**
> Trastevere is a vibrant and charming neighborhood in Rome with a lot of character. It has cobblestone streets, great restaurants, and a lively atmosphere. It's a great choice for travelers who want an authentic Roman experience. There are plenty of hotels and B&Bs to choose from.

*Why it fails: Every descriptor is generic, no tradeoffs, no specific landmarks, no transport detail, no price context, no "who it suits / doesn't suit," no verifiable local detail.*

**Strong:**
> **Trastevere** — Rome's old working-class district across the Tiber, now a tangle of ivy-covered lanes that empty into Piazza Santa Maria at night.
>
> *Who it suits:* Couples, food-focused travelers, anyone who wants to walk home from dinner rather than catch a cab. *Who it doesn't:* Light sleepers and early risers — the bars around Piazza Trilussa stay loud until 2 a.m. on weekends, and delivery trucks start rattling the cobbles around 6 a.m.
>
> *Getting around:* No Metro stop. Tram 8 from Piazza Venezia drops you at Viale Trastevere in about 10 minutes; a 20-minute walk gets you to the Pantheon over Ponte Sisto.
>
> *Safety:* Well-trafficked and well-lit on the main squares and Via della Lungaretta until late. The quieter lanes south of Viale Trastevere feel lonelier after midnight — fine, but not where you'd want to be lost.
>
> *Price tier:* Midrange dominates ($120–220/night for a double). A few boutique options in converted palazzi push past $350; genuine budget is rare here — head to Monti or Termini for under $100.
>
> *Local tip:* If you can, book something west of Viale di Trastevere. East of the main road is closer to the tram but louder and more tourist-saturated; the western lanes around Piazza San Cosimato still have a residential rhythm, with a morning market Monday through Saturday.

*Why it works: Specific landmarks (Piazza Santa Maria, Ponte Sisto, Piazza San Cosimato), specific transport (Tram 8, 10 minutes), honest tradeoffs (noise, no Metro), operationalized safety, concrete price tier with an alternative, and a verifiable local detail (the market) that a scrape wouldn't produce.*
