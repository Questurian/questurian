## FAQ Article Guide

### Purpose & Reader Intent

A FAQ article compiles short, direct answers to common questions about a single topic. It exists to offer quick, accessible explanations for issues that don’t warrant standalone articles but are significant for user understanding. According to Mozilla’s contributor guidelines, FAQ articles address recurring inquiries and aim to provide users with concise information in one convenient location.

### Core Content Expectations

- **List of relevant questions:** Identify the most frequently asked questions through user feedback, support tickets or community forums.
    
- **Concise answers:** Provide clear, to‑the‑point responses.
    
- **Logical organization:** Arrange questions in a sequence that makes sense—group by theme or move from general to specific.
    
- **Descriptive headings:** Use the questions themselves as section headings (usually H2). This helps users scan and find relevant information quickly.
    
- **Further reading:** Where appropriate, cite in‑depth articles, documentation or external sources for readers seeking more detail.
    

### Optional Enhancing Elements

- **Brief introduction or summary:** A short opening paragraph can define the overall topic and set expectations for the reader.
    
- **Grouping by subtopics:** For long FAQs, consider adding sub‑headers or categories to improve navigation.
    
- **Contextual notes:** Where needed, add brief contextual statements explaining why a question arises (e.g., after a software update or feature change).
    

### Signs the Source Material Is Insufficient

- Few or no questions posed; conversation lacks clear Q&A structure or repeated inquiries.
    
- Answers ramble without clear resolution or are overly technical with no simplification.
    
- The order of questions feels random, causing confusion.
    
- The source material repeats the same question without adding new information, indicating a lack of variety in inquiries.
    
- No references to sources or further reading when the topic clearly demands more depth.
    

### Editorial Flexibility Notes

- Tone should be succinct and neutral.
    
- Writers may choose to group questions by theme or chronology; both are acceptable if the logic is clear to readers
