## Use when

Use for a specific, recent development whose timing matters: a route launch, policy change, closure, opening, disruption, court ruling, public warning, or other verifiable event. The commission should identify the event, affected people, location, and publication moment.

## Do not use when

Do not use for evergreen advice, speculative trend pieces, or broad destination summaries. If the event only supplies a hook for a deeper argument, use Analysis. If readers mainly need background, use Explainer.

## Reader promise

State what happened, when, where, who is affected, why it matters, and what remains uncertain. Separate confirmed facts from claims, projections, and reaction. Make the newest verified information easy to find.

## Required evidence

Require at least one authoritative or firsthand source for the central event, plus independent reporting or documentation when available. The evidence record preserves timestamps, effective dates, jurisdiction, named speakers, and quotation attribution. Named people and institutions who act in the story are the story and stay in the prose; the outlet that reported it does not. For an evolving story, identify the reporting cutoff. Never turn an announcement into an accomplished fact.

## Allowed structures

1. News lead, verified details, impact, background, next developments.
2. What changed, who is affected, practical consequences, unanswered questions.
3. Chronology of a fast-moving event, followed by current status and reader action.

Keep background subordinate to the news. Use chronological order only when it clarifies cause or changing facts.

## Failure modes

Avoid stale present-tense claims, buried dates, anonymous authority, invented reaction, false balance, promotional framing, and predictions presented as outcomes. Do not pad thin news with generic destination copy. Flag conflicts and unknowns plainly.

## Headline note

Name the development and affected place or group. Use a date only when it prevents ambiguity. Avoid “everything you need to know” unless the report genuinely covers the full practical impact.
