## Use when

Use when the commission asks for an editorial judgment based on a real evaluation. The subject, evaluator, date, method, audience, and criteria should be known. Reviews may cover lodging, tours, transport products, restaurants, attractions, or travel equipment.

## Do not use when

Do not use from marketing copy, aggregated ratings, or general web research alone. Do not imply firsthand use where none exists. Use Service Guide for choosing among documented options or Feature/Profile for a reported subject.

## Reader promise

Tell readers what was evaluated, under what conditions, what worked, what failed, who it suits, and whether it delivers reasonable value. Separate observed performance from published specifications and third-party claims.

## Required evidence

Require firsthand notes or a documented evaluation record attributable to the reviewer. Record date, version or offering, price paid or provided access, conditions, and relevant limitations. Verify specifications, policies, and current public pricing independently. Disclose complimentary access, affiliate interest, or unusual treatment. Never invent testing, sensory experience, staff interaction, durability, or long-term performance.

## Allowed structures

1. Verdict, evaluation context, strengths, weaknesses, value, best fit.
2. Experience chronology, criterion-based assessment, alternatives, conclusion.
3. What was promised, what was observed, practical drawbacks, recommendation by audience.

Apply the same declared criteria throughout. Comparisons to alternatives remain contextual unless approved as a Comparison commission.

## Failure modes

Avoid promotional recap, star ratings without a rubric, universal recommendations, recency ambiguity, and treating one visit as permanent truth. Do not omit material failures or disclosure. If the evaluation record cannot support the promised judgment, stop with a research gap.

## Headline note

Name the exact subject and useful verdict or question. Avoid “honest review,” which should be assumed, and avoid claims of testing duration not documented in evidence.
