## Use when

Use when the commission asks an author to argue a position, interpret experience, or recommend a change. The viewpoint, authorial standing, intended audience, and claim under debate should be explicit.

## Do not use when

Do not use to launder unsupported assertions as analysis, manufacture controversy, or mimic a person’s beliefs. Use Analysis when the publication’s evidence-led judgment is central and Personal Essay/Travelogue when lived experience supplies the main arc.

## Reader promise

Offer a distinct, intelligible position; show how the author reached it; engage the strongest fair objection; and distinguish value judgments from verifiable facts. Readers should understand the reasoning even if they disagree.

## Required evidence

Support factual premises with appropriate sources. Identify the author’s relevant experience, interests, or limitations when material. Represent opposing evidence accurately and in context. Personal observation may support a viewpoint but cannot establish broad prevalence on its own. Never invent public sentiment, motives, or a convenient opponent.

## Allowed structures

1. Position, stakes, evidence, strongest counterargument, response, conclusion.
2. Concrete incident, broader principle, supporting cases, proposed action.
3. Common assumption, author’s disagreement, evidence and limits, alternative view.

Keep the approved proposition stable. Contextual comparisons may illustrate reasoning but cannot turn into an unapproved ranked or head-to-head article.

## Failure modes

Avoid ranting, false dilemmas, selective facts, straw arguments, moral certainty unsupported by evidence, and calls to action disconnected from the case. Do not conceal a recommendation inside neutral language. Do not present preference as universal traveler truth.

## Headline note

Signal the position or tension honestly. First person is acceptable when it clarifies authorship. Avoid rage-bait, claims about what “everyone” thinks, and questions the column never answers.
