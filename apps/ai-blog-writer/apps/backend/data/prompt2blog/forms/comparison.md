## Use when

Use only when the commission explicitly approves `head_to_head` or `ranked_set` scope and names every comparator. The reader must face a real choice, and the criteria must reflect that reader’s priorities.

## Do not use when

Do not use because another place appears as context, a benchmark, or a source example. A `single_subject` commission cannot become Comparison. Use Analysis when one primary subject is being tested and other places merely calibrate evidence.

## Reader promise

Compare like with like, show meaningful differences, explain tradeoffs, and help the primary audience choose without pretending one answer fits everyone. Preserve the approved comparator set and roles.

## Required evidence

Require comparable evidence for every subject and load-bearing criterion. Align periods, currencies, geographic units, service levels, and definitions. Explain conversions and methodological mismatches. Current evidence for one subject cannot be paired silently with old or anecdotal evidence for another. State where comparison is impossible or confidence differs.

## Allowed structures

1. Reader decision, criteria, criterion-by-criterion comparison, best fit by scenario.
2. Short subject profiles, shared scorecard in prose, tradeoffs, qualified verdict.
3. Ranked set with disclosed weighting, evidence notes, and alternatives by priority.

Organize around criteria, not separate promotional mini-guides, unless short profiles are required for orientation.

## Failure modes

Avoid false equivalence, shifting criteria, hidden weighting, universal winners, cherry-picked prices, and expanding the comparator set during research or writing. Context-only references never receive equal sections or verdicts. Missing comparable evidence must remain a visible gap.

## Headline note

Name the approved subjects and decision. “Versus” is appropriate only for true head-to-head scope. Do not imply a decisive winner when the result depends on reader priorities.
