## Use when

Use when the commission’s main promise is what a trip, stay, move, or activity costs. Define destination, period, duration, party size, travel style, currency, and inclusions. This form may model several realistic budgets without ranking destinations.

## Do not use when

Do not use for general affordability commentary, a service choice, or a multi-place value contest. Use Analysis, Service Guide, or Comparison. A few price examples do not constitute a budget breakdown.

## Reader promise

Show where money goes, what assumptions drive totals, which costs are fixed or variable, and where readers can adjust. Give ranges and scenarios when precision would mislead.

## Required evidence

Source every material price or calculation input. Record currency, tax and fee treatment, unit, booking or observation date, travel period, and conversion date. Distinguish advertised prices, typical observed prices, averages, medians, and estimates. Show arithmetic or enough inputs to reproduce totals. Include irregular costs only when the commission defines them.

## Allowed structures

1. Assumptions, category breakdown, daily and total scenarios, savings levers, uncertainty.
2. Fixed costs, variable costs, sample budgets by travel style, sensitivity check.
3. One realistic budget diary, category analysis, alternative scenarios, planning range.

Keep categories mutually understandable and avoid double counting. Explain whether transport to the destination is included.

## Failure modes

Avoid false precision, silent currency conversion, mixing dates or service levels, cherry-picked bargain prices, unsupported “average” claims, and presenting a promotional rate as normal. Never fabricate bridge values to complete a total; report a gap or partial estimate.

## Headline note

Name destination or task, duration, audience when relevant, and the pricing period. Use a number only when its assumptions are visible and current.
