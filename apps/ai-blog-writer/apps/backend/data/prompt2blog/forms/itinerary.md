## Use when

Use when the reader wants a day-by-day or stop-by-stop plan. The commission must specify destination, duration, primary audience, pace, and important constraints such as arrival time, mobility, budget, opening days, or transport mode.

## Do not use when

Do not use for a loose list of attractions, a destination overview, or a route that lacks evidence for timing and feasibility. Use Curated List/Best Of or Destination Guide instead. Do not fabricate a schedule from approximate distances.

## Reader promise

Provide a coherent plan that a real person could follow, with sensible grouping, travel time, recovery time, booking needs, and alternatives. Explain why each stop belongs and what can be dropped.

## Required evidence

Verify opening schedules, reservation requirements, seasonal access, travel times, route connections, and geographic order. Use current official sources for operational details and mapping or operator evidence for movement. Mark estimates and variability. Respect meal times, check-in constraints, daylight, altitude, weather, accessibility, and weekly closures when relevant.

## Allowed structures

1. Day-by-day plan with morning, afternoon, evening, logistics, and optional swap.
2. Base-and-excursion plan grouped by geography, with arrival and departure days.
3. Stop-by-stop route with duration ranges, transfer notes, and contingency branches.

Sequence comes from feasibility and reader priorities, not from source order or venue popularity.

## Failure modes

Avoid impossible transfers, overpacked days, unexplained backtracking, exact minute schedules without support, and treating optional stops as mandatory. Do not hide booking or mobility barriers. If critical operational facts are missing, return a research gap instead of a plausible itinerary.

## Headline note

State destination and duration. Add pace or audience only when it materially distinguishes the plan. Avoid “perfect itinerary”; travel conditions vary.
