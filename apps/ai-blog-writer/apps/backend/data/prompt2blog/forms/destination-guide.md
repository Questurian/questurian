## Use when

Use when readers need broad orientation to a destination before or during a visit. The commission must bound the geography and traveler context. A destination guide may connect neighborhoods, timing, transport, costs, customs, and major experiences without becoming an exhaustive encyclopedia.

## Do not use when

Do not use for a single practical problem, a day-by-day plan, or a ranked list. Use Service Guide, Itinerary, or Curated List/Best Of. Do not use when the title asks a narrow analytical question.

## Reader promise

Help the primary audience understand the destination, make core planning decisions, avoid consequential mistakes, and know what deserves further research. Maintain the named destination as the primary subject throughout.

## Required evidence

Verify entry basics, local movement, meaningful geographic distinctions, timing, closures, and safety or cost claims included by the commission. Prefer official and current sources for changeable facts. Explain who a recommendation suits and its tradeoffs. Context-only places may orient readers but cannot become parallel guide sections.

## Allowed structures

1. Orientation, when to go, where to base, what to experience, logistics, practical cautions.
2. Key planning decisions, area guide, daily rhythms, transport, budget, responsible travel.
3. First-visit priorities, destination layout, booking decisions, common mistakes, next steps.

Choose sections from the approved modules and requirements. Do not force every travel topic into every guide.

## Failure modes

Avoid generic praise, exhaustive attraction inventories, timeless claims about changing details, unsupported superlatives, and advice without traveler fit. Do not flatten distinct neighborhoods or cultures into stereotypes. Flag missing operational facts instead of filling them with plausible travel copy.

## Headline note

Name the bounded destination and planning promise. Add audience, season, or trip context only when central. Avoid “ultimate” and “complete” unless the scope truly warrants them.
