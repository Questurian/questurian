## Use when

Use when the reader needs to complete a specific task: apply, book, pack, configure, prepare, submit, transfer, or verify. The commission should identify the starting state, successful end state, audience, jurisdiction or destination, and important constraints.

## Do not use when

Do not use for conceptual understanding, broad planning, or a choice among many services. Use Explainer, Destination Guide, or Service Guide. A checklist alone is not a substitute for missing procedural evidence.

## Reader promise

Provide a reliable sequence, required inputs, decision points, warnings, and completion check. Readers should know what to prepare, what to do, what can fail, and how to confirm success.

## Required evidence

Use current official instructions or primary operator documentation for mandatory steps, forms, fees, deadlines, and eligibility. Verify links, names, order, and jurisdiction. Supplement with specialist or firsthand evidence only for practical friction, not to override authoritative requirements. Identify effective and retrieval dates. Mark optional steps and paths that vary by reader.

## Allowed structures

1. Before you start, numbered steps, troubleshooting, completion check.
2. Checklist grouped by timing: weeks before, day before, day of, after.
3. Decision tree with separate verified paths, then a shared final check.

Keep each step atomic and outcome-focused. Place warnings immediately before the risky action.

## Failure modes

Avoid missing prerequisites, vague verbs, mixed jurisdictions, obsolete screenshots or labels, hidden fees, and false guarantees. Do not invent a workaround. If one critical step cannot be verified, expose the gap rather than publishing a broken sequence.

## Headline note

Lead with the task and relevant place, system, or deadline. “Step by step” is useful only when the article contains a genuine ordered process.
