# Prompt2Blog headline standard

## Source of truth

Start from the original title and approved commission. Preserve the primary subject, location, direction, scope, and reader promise. Use the final verified article to sharpen accuracy, never to introduce a broader subject or more dramatic conclusion.

## Core standard

A headline should identify the subject and communicate the article’s useful tension, answer, task, or selection. Prefer concrete nouns and active language. Include destination, duration, audience, date, or price only when each materially defines the promise. Keep context-only references out unless their mention is necessary to understand the approved question.

## Accuracy

Match certainty to evidence. A question headline must receive a clear, qualified answer. Numbers, rankings, “best,” “cheapest,” “safest,” “new,” and “still” require direct support and a relevant time frame. Do not turn association into cause or a partial finding into a universal verdict.

## Clarity

Avoid duplicate title and deck ideas, stacked clauses, unexplained acronyms, vague pronouns, and empty labels such as “ultimate guide.” Do not use clickbait withholding, false urgency, rage-bait, or “everything you need to know.” A reader should understand what the article covers before opening it.

## Form notes

Apply the selected form’s short headline note. Form can shape phrasing but cannot change commission scope. Comparison headlines name only approved comparators. Review headlines identify the evaluated subject. Interview headlines quote only attributable words. Itinerary and budget headlines state the duration or assumptions that make the promise meaningful.

## Final check

Confirm headline uses original intent, names the right primary subject, makes no unsupported claim, excludes unapproved comparators, and accurately represents what the article delivers.
