## Research questions

What dishes, drinks, markets, producers, customs, or venues answer the commission? What makes them relevant to this audience? Which claims concern history, identity, quality, price, dietary fit, reservation, or current operation?

## Preferred sources

Use official venue information for hours and policies, menus for dated price examples, food authorities, producers, scholars, reputable culinary reporting, and attributable firsthand material for taste or service judgments. Aggregated reviews can reveal questions, not establish facts alone.

## Freshness

Recheck operation, chef or ownership claims, menus, prices, reservations, and opening schedules. Cultural and historical claims still require credible sourcing even when less volatile.

## Factual limits and gaps

Never claim the writer tasted, visited, or experienced something without supplied firsthand evidence. Avoid “authentic,” “best,” and origin stories without definition and support. Note dietary cross-contact uncertainty. Warn when venue status, a quoted price, attribution, or a promised firsthand evaluation is missing.
