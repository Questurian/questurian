## Research questions

Which behavior, setting, community, language, ceremony, or historical context matters to the commission? What varies by generation, region, faith, relationship, or formality? What should a visitor ask, observe, avoid, or reciprocate rather than assume?

## Preferred sources

Use local scholars, cultural institutions, community organizations, language authorities, reputable local reporting, and attributable local or firsthand voices. Tourism marketing may identify topics but should not define cultural truth alone.

## Freshness

Historical context can be durable; current terminology, laws, contested practices, community preferences, and event protocols require recent verification.

## Factual limits and gaps

Avoid universal claims about what locals think, authenticity tests, exoticism, and etiquette lists detached from context. Distinguish legal requirements, formal protocol, common courtesy, and one person’s preference. Warn when the commission promises community perspective without attributable voices or when a contested claim has only one institutional viewpoint.
