## Research questions

Which areas fit the primary audience, and why? What are the tradeoffs in access, atmosphere, noise, mobility, cost, and daily needs? What lodging types, restrictions, booking patterns, or seasonal pressures matter? Are named boundaries official, customary, or platform-defined?

## Preferred sources

Use municipal maps and planning sources, official lodging rules, transport authorities, current operator or property information, reputable local reporting, and attributable firsthand evidence. Booking platforms may show examples but not prove typical prices or neighborhood character.

## Freshness

Verify current operation, rules, transit access, and material price claims. Neighborhood history may use older sources; practical recommendations need recent support.

## Factual limits and gaps

Avoid declaring an area universally best, safe, authentic, quiet, or walkable. State audience fit and tradeoffs. Do not flatten disputed boundaries or infer lived conditions from listings. Warn when accommodation legality, accessibility, operational status, or a load-bearing neighborhood claim cannot be verified.
