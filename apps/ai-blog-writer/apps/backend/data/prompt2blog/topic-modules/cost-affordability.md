## Research questions

What costs matter to the primary audience? What period, geography, currency, unit, party size, and standard of service make figures comparable? Which costs changed, and what explains the change? Does “affordable” mean low absolute price, value for quality, or affordability relative to income?

## Preferred sources

Use official tariffs, operator prices, government statistics, reputable market datasets, dated menus or booking evidence, and methodologically transparent reporting. Use firsthand observations as examples, not market averages.

## Freshness

Treat prices, exchange rates, rents, and fees as volatile. Record observation and conversion dates. Older trend series may establish direction but cannot substitute for a current planning figure.

## Factual limits and gaps

Never mix currencies, periods, neighborhoods, taxes, or service levels silently. Label advertised, observed, average, median, and modeled figures accurately. Do not infer a citywide cost from one listing. Warn when essential categories lack current evidence, samples are not comparable, or a claimed bargain depends on an exceptional rate.
