## Research questions

What hazards are relevant to the approved place, activity, time, and audience? What is the likelihood, severity, and practical mitigation? Which risks vary by neighborhood, season, transport mode, identity, health status, or behavior? What can official evidence establish, and what remains uncertain?

## Preferred sources

Use local authorities, emergency and health agencies, official advisories, methodologically sound crime or incident data, transport operators, specialist safety bodies, and careful local reporting. Firsthand accounts illustrate conditions but cannot establish prevalence.

## Freshness

Verify alerts, closures, health guidance, laws, and operational risks near publication. Historical data needs dates, definitions, and geographic fit.

## Factual limits and gaps

Never label a whole city or neighborhood safe or unsafe without qualification. Avoid fearmongering, reassurance beyond evidence, and cross-city crime comparisons built from incompatible data. Give actionable precautions without blaming victims. Warn when promised risk comparisons, emergency details, or claims about safer areas lack comparable support.
