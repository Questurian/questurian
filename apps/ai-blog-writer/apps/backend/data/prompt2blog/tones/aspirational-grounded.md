## Aspirational but Grounded Tone

Best for destination openers, lifestyle sections, and pieces whose job is to make somewhere feel worth considering.

Commits to: the place being worth it, and to a reason for every part of that claim. Tie each one to something concrete: a cost, a season, a dish, a working setup, a neighbourhood, an access route, a daily friction that turns out to be smaller than expected. Desire without an anchor is advertising.

The writer is invisible. The appeal comes from the specifics, never from the writer's excitement about them.

This is the easiest tone in the set to get wrong, because the pull toward brochure language is strongest here and the house rules ban exactly that vocabulary. Every image must be true and traceable to the evidence. No invented sensory detail, no vague transformation claims, and no line that could describe forty other places. If a claim cannot be anchored, cut it rather than reaching for an adjective.

Not this tone if the piece has to weigh the place against alternatives, in which case use Practical Authority.
