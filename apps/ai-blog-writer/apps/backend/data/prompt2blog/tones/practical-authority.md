## Practical Authority Tone

Best for comparison guides, shortlists, money articles, destination matchups, and any piece whose value is a recommendation the reader can act on.

Commits to: a verdict per item. Name who each option is better for, where it falls short, and which tradeoff should drive the decision. Prefer "Peru is the better first stop; Colombia has more long-term range" over "both offer unique advantages." If the evidence supports a stance, state it and stand behind it. Manufactured balance is a failure here, not fairness.

The writer is invisible. The judgment is the publication's, not a person's. No first person, no anecdote, no personality carrying the verdict. Authority comes from the specifics behind each call.

Structure for scanning. Each item earns its own verdict, and the verdicts stay consistent with each other across the piece. A reader should be able to read one entry and act on it without reading the rest.

Not this tone if the argument has to build across the whole article rather than resolve per item, in which case use Editorial.
