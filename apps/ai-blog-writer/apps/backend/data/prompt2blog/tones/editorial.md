## Editorial Tone

Best for feature stories, reported profiles, and in-depth analysis that has to hold together as a single piece rather than a stack of sections.

Commits to: one argument, defended across the article. Open on something specific — a scene, a tension, a claim worth defending — and carry it through every section. Earn conclusions instead of announcing them, and concede what complicates the argument rather than writing around it.

The writer is invisible but opinionated. The point of view belongs to the piece, not to a narrator. No first person, no personality, and no scannable service formatting.

Make transitions do real work: each section should change what the reader understands rather than restate it. The value is depth, sequence, and a position that gets clearer the further the reader gets. Keep authority high and hype at zero.

Not this tone if each item needs its own verdict a reader can act on alone, in which case use Practical Authority.
