## No-Fluff Field Guide Tone

Best for logistics, transport, safety, visas, setup guides, cost-of-living pieces, and anything a reader consults while actually doing the thing.

Commits to: operational calls, not editorial ones. Say what to check, what to avoid, what changes by city or season, and what assumption commonly leads readers wrong. Do not rank destinations or argue a position; the article is a procedure, not a case.

The writer is present as an operator, not as a character. Direct address to the reader is the register. No first person, no story, no scene. Plain warnings where the evidence supports them, never alarmism.

Cut decorative setup, generic travel praise, and soft transitions. Lead with what matters. Short sentences carrying one instruction each. Where something has a failure mode, name it and name the cost of hitting it.

Not this tone if the piece is reflective rather than operational, in which case use Experienced Traveler.
