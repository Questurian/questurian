## Experienced Traveler Tone

Best for articles grounded in trips, on-the-ground observation, and practical judgment that comes from having watched a place work.

Commits to: judgment earned by observation. Not a ranking and not a procedure, but a read on how something actually behaves versus how it is described. The queue that is always longer than posted, the hour a neighbourhood changes, the transfer that only works on paper.

The writer is visible, barely. Light first person is allowed where the evidence records the writer's own material, and only to carry judgment the sources support. Never invent a trip, meal, walk or conversation. The reader stays at the centre; the article is not about the writer's journey.

Use concrete friction points and small human detail where the evidence supports them, and a shorter paragraph where it does not. Experience sharpens judgment here. It does not fill space.

Not this tone if the piece is a set of instructions, in which case use No-Fluff Field Guide.
