The verdict: partial success

The plan said in advance what this outcome would be called: “If only menu descriptions improve while recommendation evidence stays thin, report partial success and specify next missing evidence.” That is what happened.

What genuinely improved. Attribution, completely. Every claim now points at an address a reader can open, with the sentence that carries it and the page's own date. The menu material is far better: twelve named La Casa flavours at S/ 25.00 from the restaurant's own site, the wings smoked rather than fried, six pieces with chips and a house salad; McCarthy's thirteen sauces with two of them named. Two claims were caught and flagged rather than stored as facts.

What did not improve. The thing the list's own standard is written around. Its words are “Lima food writers, bloggers or recent customer reviews that single out the wings”, and across three places the pilot found none. 3 claims count as attributable to somebody other than the business, and all 3 come from a single trade-press launch article dated 2020-12-28 — a chain announcing its arrival, not anybody eating there. Zero customer reviews, zero food writing, at any of the three. Whether that is because the searching is still wrong or because the writing does not exist is not settled by three requests.

What is missing, in the order it should be answered

  1. A dated, attributable opinion about the wings. Not one of the three has one. Review platforms carry them and every one this run touched refused the reader (Restaurant Guru 404, PedidosYa 403, Mercado Negro 403). A reader that can read one review platform is worth more than a fourth search.
  2. The dine-in price at La Casa's Los Olivos branch. The S/ 25.00 on the brand site has no channel and no branch; the extraction said so.
  3. Whether McCarthy's thirteen sauces and its Monday promotion still hold. Both are 2020 facts. A current menu page would settle them and none was reachable.
  4. BarBarian, re-run. Its packet is empty for a reason that was this reader's fault, not the design's — see below.

Two defects this pilot found, both fixed, neither re-bought

  • A host with no DNS record was reported as an address the reader refused. BarBarian's search returned www.barbarian.com.pe, which does not exist; the reader said it “does not resolve to a public address”, which reads as a safety refusal and sends the next person looking for the wrong problem. A dead link and a private address are now two different states. (The real site is barbarian.pe, and it reads.)
  • A redirect that lands on an index page was recorded as a successful read of the page asked for. Rappi answers an unknown menu slug with its restaurant directory, at 200. BarBarian's one readable page was that directory, which is why its extraction honestly reported finding nothing. A page that redirects away from the address requested now says so.

Neither fix has been paid for. Re-running BarBarian would be a fourth grounded search, and the authorisation was three. It is the obvious next spend and it is a decision, not a consequence.

What this does not claim

  • A passage check is not a truth check. It proves the sentence is in the page. The page can be wrong, the menu can be stale, and a business flattering itself passes every check here.
  • Two generations and eight pages are a budget chosen against the shape of the material. Three requests do not measure whether they are the right numbers.
  • The baseline and the retest ran a day apart against a live web. Some of the difference is the web, not the design.

“Passed a passage check” means the quoted sentence was found in a page this process fetched and stored. It is not a claim that the sentence is true, that the page is honest, or that the menu is current.
