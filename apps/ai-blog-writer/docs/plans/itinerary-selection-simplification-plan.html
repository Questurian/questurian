01 / THE DECISION

A meaningful rewrite, mostly by subtraction.

Keep the trip setup, day selection, skeletons, and useful grill conversation. Change what the next stage is responsible for: select places that work together. Stop asking it to simultaneously produce finished prose, prove every editorial choice, and give the editor a research review job.

The result should answer three questions.

Where are we going? Why these places? How does this itinerary fit together?

This is bigger than hiding badges or shortening a prompt. Selection instructions, saved results, and the result screen must agree on the smaller job. It does not require rebuilding the whole app or the separate article-writing workflow.

03 / THE PROPOSED EXPERIENCE

From your idea to a proposal you can judge.

Describe trip→Arrange days + stays→Grill→Choose places→Review proposal

1. Describe the trip

Keep the current basic setup. Title and preferences tell AI what kind of itinerary to make. An empty preference remains unspecified; it does not create a new requirement.

2. Arrange the days

Keep slots, their order, and optional stops. Each slot needs only its role and any meaningful preference. “Small local snack” is sufficient; no essay about how snacks relate to lunch.

3. Discuss what matters

Keep the grill interaction. Its final summary should be short enough to scan: the trip’s angle, important day differences, and explicit constraints. Separate firm user requirements from suggestions AI can adjust. A preference like “walkable” must not silently become a rigid fifteen-minute rule.

4. Let AI make the proposal

AI chooses places around the stay, slot roles, title, geography, opening times, and other days. It replaces unsuitable candidates during research. It can use judgment within the agreed skeleton without reporting every rejected option.

5. Review the choices

Show the whole trip at a glance, then each day’s places. Each place gets one short selection reason. Let the editor keep it, swap it, or request a revision. No claim-review checklist or “read every source” requirement.

04 / WHAT DISAPPEARS — WHAT STILL MATTERS

Research remains. The review ceremony goes.

Remove from this stage

  • Finished day intros and reader-facing stop blurbs.
  • House voice and article-writing rules in the selection prompt.
  • Repeated “why here,” “what to do,” and selection explanations.
  • Routine red/yellow editorial grades and feasibility essays.
  • Manual source-review checkbox and its unchecked status.
  • Review notes as a required step, redundant gates, and generic caution lists.
  • Minute-by-minute schedules unless a real booking or timed activity needs them.

Keep behind the scenes

  • Correct venue identity, branch, city, and location.
  • Research for opening days and facts that affect the choice.
  • Reasonable route and travel estimates, labeled as estimates.
  • Sources available through optional details.
  • No duplicates across days unless intentional.
  • Saved work, retries, cancellation, ownership checks, and protection against stale responses.
  • Basic checks that a response belongs to this trip and fills the expected slots.

05 / HOTELS WITHOUT A SECOND PRODUCT

Choose a stay alongside the day skeleton.

Add “Where you’re staying” to the skeleton stage, once days exist. Two selection methods: pick from Location Manager hotels for the overnight city, or let AI recommend a hotel.

  • Apply a stay to a range of nights. Following days reuse it by default.
  • Track where the day starts and where the traveler sleeps that night. They may differ.
  • A day trip keeps the existing hotel. An overnight getaway can introduce another stay.
  • The final day does not automatically require another hotel night.
  • A recommended hotel is a suggestion, never a booking or a claim of live availability.
  • AI resolves recommendations before finalizing routes; start/end journeys use the selected stay.
  • Show the stay once with its night range, and compact references on affected days.

Use existing hotel records and lookup plumbing where suitable. The separate older autobuild pipeline already has lodging selection, but that is not a reason to bring its scoring/reporting system into this workflow. Budget comparison, booking integrations, and hotel inventory management are outside this change.

06 / A SMALLER ASSIGNMENT

Tell AI what to choose, not how to write an article.

Choose places for this itinerary using the title, agreed preferences, day skeletons, and overnight stays. Fill each slot with a suitable place or experience. Make each day geographically sensible and avoid unintended repetition across days. Research facts that affect whether a choice works; replace unsuitable candidates. Return each choice with a short reason, plus a brief overview of how the trip fits together. Ask only about conflicts that require changing an explicit request. Do not write article blurbs.

This is the plain-language core, not the complete production prompt. The actual call adds the trip details once, the slots once, compact prior-day choices, and a small response format.

Result information to retain

Trip: short overall rationale. Day: short route/selection summary and stay reference. Slot: place name, identity/location, category, brief reason, and any truly useful practical note. Supporting details: sources and estimated journeys. Exceptions: only unresolved choices needing the editor.

Aim for one sentence per place reason and two or three sentences per day overview. These guide brevity; they are not editorial approval gates. Measure full prompt size including the response format. Initial engineering target: a normal seven-slot day below 10,000 characters, excluding unusually long explicit user content. This is a target to test, not a proven limit or a reason to truncate user requirements.

  1. Define the smaller result

    Introduce a selection-specific contract: picks, short reasons, day/trip summary, stay references, optional evidence, and genuine decision questions. Stop using the article-shaped day result as the new output. Establish what is a user requirement versus an adjustable preference.

    Touchpoints: backend contracts.py, research_contract.py; frontend dayWork/types.ts.

  2. Add stays to the skeleton

    Add Location Manager selection and AI recommendation mode, night ranges, inheritance, and explicit hotel changes. Resolve arrival/final-day cases. Persist stays with setup and include them in change detection.

    Touchpoints: frontend types.ts, setupReducer.ts, skeleton components; backend SetupSnapshot and context.py; existing Location Manager accommodation integration.

  3. Replace the research assignment

    Keep interview answers, condense accepted intent into the smaller brief, remove voice/blurbs and duplicated checks. Select the stay before final route choices. Research replacements internally. Keep both in-app generation and copy/paste import working against the same selection format.

    Touchpoints: direction.py, grill_prompt.py, prompt_export.py, research.py, research_adapter.py, context.py.

  4. Replace the result screen and state logic

    Render whole-trip overview, per-day picks, one reason each, stay information, and optional details. Provide targeted swaps and day revisions. Recompute affected route/continuity when a place or stay changes. Remove personal evidence-review state and routine editorial blocking; retain technical integrity checks.

    Touchpoints: DayResultView.tsx, DayArticle.tsx, AttentionPanel.tsx, attention.ts, DayProgress.tsx; service.py, validation.py, API review endpoints.

  5. Preserve old work without carrying old complexity forward

    Back up stored itinerary records before any data change. Keep existing results readable as previous-version records. Start new generation from setup and the shorter agreed brief; do not silently regenerate, delete, or pretend old blurbs are the new selections. Old imported answers must be identified clearly. Remove obsolete generation branches after the transition, leaving only the minimal old-record reader needed to preserve work.

    Review existing uncommitted work before edits. Current workspace already contains substantial itinerary changes.

  6. Verify and hand off to later writing

    Use the saved Lima setup as the main comparison. Confirm all seven slots get picks and reasons, without article copy or review ceremony. Define a clean handoff of chosen places and context for later writing; do not build a new writing pipeline in this scope.

    Update itinerary contract/import/workflow tests and frontend day-work tests. Retire tests for removed review behavior.

Order matters: agree on the smaller result first; build stays and selection around it; replace UI/state together. A visual cleanup alone would leave the same oversized assignment underneath.

08 / ACCEPTANCE

We are done when the proposal is easy to judge.

  • The Lima example shows seven slot choices, one short reason each, and a short day overview.
  • No generated article intros, reader blurbs, feasibility essays, or source-review checkbox.
  • Title, real preferences, skeleton order, optionality, and cross-day continuity survive simplification.
  • A closed AI-selected venue is replaced; an impossible user-required venue produces one actionable question.
  • Unspecified access/diet fields create neither invented requirements nor false suitability claims.
  • Hotel selection works from Location Manager and AI recommendation; same-hotel days, overnight changes, and final-day departure work.
  • Sources remain available without dominating the proposal.
  • Copy/import and in-app generation save the same selection result; wrong-trip or stale responses cannot overwrite current work.
  • Changing a place or hotel updates affected planning without discarding unrelated accepted choices.
  • Prompt size and generation time are measured against the existing example; required user information is never silently cut.
  • Old saved results remain accessible. No article-writing or publishing step runs automatically.
Recommended scope

One focused rewrite of the day-selection workflow, plus a small stay control. Preserve setup and useful infrastructure. Remove the extra writing and review responsibilities.
