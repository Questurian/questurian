A better day.
A smaller assignment.

The decision

One research call. One coherent day. Enough evidence to trust the recommendation, enough prose to make the article worth reading.

Reduce the assignment rather than introduce a new pipeline. Keep the existing setup → Grill → accepted direction → research → review/save workflow. The research call still selects places, checks the route, and writes the day. No separate planner, verifier, writer, automatic repair call, or additional approval stage.

“One call” means one application-level research invocation with web tools. It cannot mean one provider round trip: searches and page reads require additional turns inside that invocation. The existing Grill and direction extraction calls remain; this plan does not add another.

Success: a seven-stop day receives roughly 12,000–18,000 characters of application-owned instructions, context and compact schema, instead of the current ~49,000-character export; returns useful day prose and concise supporting facts; finishes with substantially fewer searches. These are implementation targets, not measured savings.

This is a proposed implementation plan. Only this HTML document has been created. No application code, database records, prompts, or saved agreements have been changed.

What makes the article better

  1. Choose for the whole day. Anchor meal or experience first, then nearby supporting stops. Geographic coherence outranks a famous venue that breaks the route.
  2. Preserve the emotional shape. The promise, anchor, pace, appetite, recovery and contrast between stops remain first-class context. Reducing prompt size must not produce a list of addresses.
  3. Research decisions, not decoration. Exact branch, location, relevant opening window, necessary booking conditions, actual adjacent journeys and explicit user requirements deserve effort. Founding dates, awards and origin stories do not get independent searches unless essential to the day’s promise.
  4. Write from the evidence already gathered. Finish within the same call. Do not reopen research merely to decorate a paragraph. Prefer a specific action and a clear reason over historical filler.
  5. Unknown is acceptable. Do not invent facts, broaden the search indefinitely, or claim universal suitability. Missing critical evidence leaves a stop unresolved; noncritical uncertainty becomes a short editor note.
  6. Checks remain honest. Structural validation is not source verification. Human evidence review remains separate from planning completeness and saving.

Reader-facing output

Keep a title, a 60–90-word introduction, and approximately 45–75 words per selected venue/experience. These are editorial guidance, not minimum padding requirements. Each stop says what to do, why it belongs here, and one concrete useful detail. Free-time/travel rows need only 15–35 words. Practical conditions stay outside the paragraph. A seven-stop day should normally deliver about 400–650 words of useful article copy.

Use the canonical Questurian Voice plus writing conventions unchanged. Remove Prompt2Blog-specific house rules from this itinerary request; retain essential evidence rules once in the itinerary instructions. Do not create a second voice or change shared article prompts.

Reduce the brief at its source

New interviews

Update itinerary-only Grill instructions: recommendations should settle one decision in roughly 40–80 words; use setup answers already supplied. Unknown dietary/access needs remain unspecified. Do not turn them into universal accommodation requirements or repeated questions. Ask only when this changes the requested audience or central experience. A general travel article can proceed without claiming allergy or mobility suitability.

Hard requirements must come from the setup or an explicitly accepted answer. “Prefer walkable” must not become “every leg ≤15 minutes”; “evergreen” must not become “every venue open seven days” unless accepted. Record operating-day restrictions instead. Count motorized neighborhood moves separately from ordinary walking legs: “one transfer” in the audited direction meant one ride, not a ban on walking between stops.

Existing direction extraction, no extra summarizer

Keep the current direction model and extraction call. Change its instructions to say each constraint once, in its owning field. Global constraints belong in constraints; slot-specific requirements in that slot; geography in geography. The research checklist adds unanswered decision-critical facts only. Do not repeat every must-have in change_policy, fails_if and checklist. Existing extraction must preserve all accepted meaning, even when it cannot fit the target.

Deterministic export projection

  • Serialize day purpose, anchor, route, rhythm and actual hard constraints into short labelled lines.
  • Render each approved slot once, combining its setup label/kind/optionality with slot-specific requirements. Keep allowed categories, travel endpoints and relevant exclusions; remove redundant cues/preferred labels when they repeat the role.
  • Deduplicate exact normalized strings only. Do not use fuzzy matching that might erase a meaningful distinction.
  • Include compact cross-day reserved themes and already selected places, rather than complete other-day descriptions.
  • Exclude agreement_trace from the sent prompt. Keep it available in direction review and saved records.

Existing overgrown agreements

Never silently remove an accepted accessibility or dietary requirement. Existing agreements that remain too large after lossless projection receive a section-size warning. Operator can run unchanged or use the existing reopen/revise-direction workflow to accept a smaller scope. For this Lima run, prepare a visible revision removing universal suitability only if accepted; retain the original agreement in history.

Two comparisons are required: same accepted direction with less duplication, then a clearly labelled revised direction. Do not attribute savings from relaxed requirements to serialization alone.

One call, bounded research

  1. Plan before searching. Read day promise, choose anchor role, sketch neighborhood progression. This is internal work in the same call, not an extra response or model invocation.
  2. Find a workable anchor. Check exact branch, relevant opening window and explicit must-haves. Explore an alternate only when first candidate fails a hard requirement.
  3. Fill supporting slots near that anchor. Prioritize route fit and contrast. Do not research multiple candidates merely to prove a recommendation was exhaustive.
  4. Check each adjacent journey. Use the actual selected endpoints. Distinguish sourced route time, conservative planning estimate and unknown. A district-level taxi estimate is not verified door-to-door timing.
  5. Resolve day-level conflicts. Check closures, meal load, rest, travel buffers and other-day repetition. If constraints cannot coexist, return a concise unresolved issue.
  6. Write once. Use gathered evidence for title, intro and stop paragraphs; then return compact JSON. No fresh searches for decorative prose.

Stopping rule

Initial budget for a seven-stop day: target 12–18 WebSearch calls and 8–12 selective WebFetch calls. Read one strong supporting page per selected venue when possible; fetch a second only for a consequential missing fact or conflict. One focused follow-up per missing critical fact, then unresolved. For venue/experience count N, guidance is min(24, 2N + 4) searches and min(18, N + 5) fetches. Free-time/travel slots do not count as venue searches; actual route checks share the budget.

These are prompt-level soft budgets, not an enforced search gate. Current transport buffers a CLI subprocess and cannot interrupt at a tool boundary while guaranteeing a final answer. Keep existing scaled timeout as a failure ceiling; do not “optimize” by killing useful work earlier. Record actual counts after completion. No automatic retry after budget overrun, timeout, malformed output or quota failure. Show failure clearly; retries remain explicit.

Hard per-tool interception or streaming recovery would be a separate transport redesign. It is excluded from this reduction. If measured runs repeatedly exceed the budget, rollout fails; tighten scope using the transcript before considering more infrastructure.

A smaller model output, existing storage

Introduce a compact itinerary-day-research-v2 wire contract for new research exports. Keep the existing saved DayResult representation. One pure adapter turns a valid compact response into that representation before existing semantic validation. This avoids rewriting result history, UI state and every downstream consumer merely to ask the model fewer questions.

Prose and practical notes may contain only established factual content. Evidence focuses on facts actually used to choose, schedule or describe the stop. Cut unsupported historical/sensory flourishes; do not excuse them from evidence. A reference is still unverified until a person checks it.

Adapter rules

  • Stamp identity from the export for in-app calls. Manual responses must echo the export envelope; validate it before adapting.
  • Generate deterministic source and claim IDs; deduplicate identical URLs without dropping significant query parameters. Same fact at different branches remains separate. Set sourceType conservatively to secondary unless the wire format carries a supported classification; never infer official status from appearance.
  • Place local evidence into existing claims/sources/stop.claimIds. Transfer evidence maps to existing source references; never manufacture routed status.
  • Derive mapsUrl as a labelled map-search link from name/address. It is a convenience link, not routing evidence.
  • Leave redundant whyHere, selectionReason and whatToDo empty. Reader paragraph contains action and fit. Do not fill them with duplicate prose just to preserve old volume.
  • Map blocking issues to unresolved feasibility; nonblocking issues to editor notes; proposed changes to existing proposals. Do not generate seven positive feasibility essays.
  • Derive tripRole from accepted direction, schedule label from setup, used places from selected stops, and remaining continuity from existing direction plus nextDayNotes.
  • Capture research date and browsing activity from the invocation when available. Missing telemetry remains unknown/unchecked, not a fabricated “no browsing.” Never infer browsing merely from more than one turn.

Nulls remain meaningful. Unresolved venue: null name/address/times, empty reader copy, explanatory reason. Free-time: no venue search/name/address, but a duration and purpose. Travel slot: one matching leg; avoid counting its duration twice. Empty array means none, not missing required work. Schema uses short descriptions and permissive values; semantic validation provides readable errors.

Compact schema must pass local schema validation and a single integration smoke before rollout. Use inline shared item definitions if the installed CLI cannot resolve local references; do not depend on remote schema URLs.

Make the work understandable

Prompt panel: show readable compact brief first, with “Instructions”, “Output format” and “Full export” disclosures. Display exact sent-character breakdown, not a wall of schema by default. Show “One research call with web searches” and expected scope. Copy/download produces the same compact contract and full identity required for manual import.

Result panel: agenda and article copy first. Relevant practical notes under each stop; adjacent transfers visibly connect the timeline. One “Needs your attention” list displays blocking issues before save, followed by nonblocking caveats. Evidence opens beside the stop it supports, with exact address and unchecked status. Preserve global sources/raw JSON/history as collapsed secondary views.

Run summary: elapsed time, recorded cost, turns, searches/fetches when known, initial input and response size. Label unavailable counts unknown. No dependency on the absent usage-monitor service for basic local evidence.

Do not invent a preview/approval checkpoint between planning and writing. Existing review/save remains the only post-call interaction. No new publisher or whole-trip article assembly stage: this scope improves the day-level prose the current feature already generates.

Saved data, versions, failures and rollback

  • No destructive migration. Existing saved DayResult records and accepted directions remain readable. New wire shape is an external request format, not a rewrite of historical results.
  • Version explicitly. Add prompt-policy/wire-version fields with defaults for existing exports. New input identity includes new wire contract version and policy revision. Existing exported requests remain immutable and are interpreted by their stored schema version.
  • Legacy export handling. Already-issued v1 clipboard answers remain importable by existing validator. New exports use compact wire format. Existing in-flight v1 calls may finish normally; do not relabel them. A rerun uses a fresh export after operator rebuilds it.
  • Minimal adapter boundary. Version dispatch happens once at import/research normalization. Do not spread dual-format branches throughout the UI. No new legacy generation option is exposed after rollout.
  • Raw vs normalized. Preserve actual compact wire response separately from normalized DayResult. Raw JSON disclosure must say which it shows. Never describe adapter-generated fields as returned verbatim by the model.
  • Local metadata. Use one additive run-details record keyed by attempt_key for sent sizes/hash, wire version, raw wire response, normalized response reference, session ID, usage, tool counts and completion state. Existing run rows remain unchanged. Missing historical metadata stays null. Back up SQLite before applying additive DDL.
  • Transcript association. Store session ID returned by CLI if present. Copy only that session’s transcript into app-owned audit storage when accessible; never search by nearby timestamps or copy unrelated sessions. Missing transcript is a telemetry gap, not a failed itinerary.
  • Privacy. Store only this run’s material locally, never credentials/environment dumps. Do not commit full user-session transcripts. No uploads required.
  • Rollback. Keep compact generator disabled until tests/smoke pass. If quality fails, revert default generation to current export policy while retaining adapter reader for already-created compact runs. Do not delete histories or re-run research automatically.

The Prompt2Blog v4 prohibition on reviving old article intake paths is untouched. This narrowly preserves existing itinerary records; it does not restore v2/v3 Prompt2Blog behavior.

Controlled research evaluation

Reuse the completed run as baseline; do not rerun the old 21-minute assignment. After offline checks, run one compact seven-stop assignment with the same accepted direction and same model/effort. Inspect actual route evidence, branch matching and schedule conditions. If the direction is then explicitly revised, run that separately and label the scope difference. Finally smoke-test one representative three-stop day. Maximum three successful new evaluations in this implementation cycle; stop on quota/transport failure or repeated quality failure instead of retrying blindly.

Before each evaluation record prompt/schema sizes, expected research allowance and exact constraints. After it, record runtime, cost, tool counts, output length and a short quality scorecard. Provider-reported cost is observational; this plan does not promise a hard dollar ceiling.

Decisions this plan closes

Keep single research/write call. Keep canonical voice. Keep article copy. Keep existing saved result model. Use one compact wire adapter. Keep sourcing but attach it to stops. Keep real constraints and human review. Remove duplicate instructions and positive feasibility essays. Use soft research budgets with honest telemetry. Do not add tool interception or a new maps provider. Do not silently revise old agreements.

The central fix: give the model fewer things to express and fewer incidental facts to chase, while making the day’s route, timing and reader value the first things it must get right.
