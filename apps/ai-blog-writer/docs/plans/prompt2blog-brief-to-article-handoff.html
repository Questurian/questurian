02 / Why we are changing direction

The old system repeatedly translates the intention: brief → work order → research notes → fact records → selection → packet → outline → compose → grounding → audit → repair → settle. Each translation can alter what matters, and each model has a different view of the assignment.

  • The work order can turn optional detail into required research. Research then asks for a full answer and says “Then keep going,” expanding beyond the question.
  • The saved Bogotá example contained 57 questions and 431 claims, with 18 selected facts. This is historical evidence of scope growth, not a current performance benchmark.
  • The saved Valparaíso run contained seven final work-order questions, 165 claims and 34 packet facts. It rejected the section outline, attempted repair, restored the earlier draft and finished needs_revision. Its ledger recorded 37 calls; repeated intake work is included.
  • The owner gave a saved Valparaíso brief plus a short writing/style prompt to Claude Opus High in a separate chat. The owner strongly prefers that article to the pipeline version. It has a more coherent four-stop itinerary and concrete descriptions.
  • That preferred draft still contains editing problems: a four-versus-seven headline ambiguity; a fare assertion stronger than its research note; a museum presented as a seating answer; excessive restoration detail. These look more tractable than rebuilding its entire structure.
  • Some pressure begins in the brief itself, especially requiring seating and hours for every lift. Nevertheless, the agreed first replacement keeps the grill/brief unchanged. Do not turn this migration into a grill redesign.
  • One preferred sample does not prove universal quality, accuracy, speed or cost gains. Treat it as the motivating baseline and evaluate a small set of fresh drafts.

The previous assessment separated editorial quality from factual support. The new article’s vivid detail is not independently verified merely because it reads better. The editor must examine source consistency as well as prose.

04 / Exact proposed writing prompt

Use the following as the initial production-intent template. It is based on the successful manual experiment, with small explicit refinements: factual claims in the brief remain checkable, the seed is not a fixed headline, optional detail should not be invented, and source-note contradictions should be resolved before returning. This revised template has not itself been tested on a model.

Write an approximately {{target_word_count}}-word article from the approved Article Brief below.

Handle the research, editorial choices and writing yourself. Research what you need to deliver this particular article. Choose the structure that best serves the reader. Spend research effort on facts that affect the reader’s decision; stop pursuing optional detail when it adds little to this article.

The brief records editorial intent, not verified facts. Verify its factual premises and practical details that could change a reader’s plans. Prefer authoritative, current sources for changing facts. Preserve meaningful dates, exceptions and uncertainty. Do not turn an old observation into a claim about today.

Where evidence contradicts the brief, preserve the reader’s purpose and use the supported facts. Never invent a detail to satisfy a requested item. If a requested detail cannot be established, avoid an unsupported assertion and identify the omission in the separate research note. State a limitation in the article when the reader needs it to act safely or make the decision.

The seed is a starting idea. Write a headline that accurately describes the finished article. Make clear what you selected versus what exists overall.

Return the headline and complete article, followed by a separate section headed exactly:
## Research note

In that note, list the source links supporting the consequential claims and identify any requested details you could not establish. Use actual sources you accessed. Keep the note outside the article body.

Before returning, read the article and research note together. Resolve contradictions between them. Ensure the headline, counts, recommendations and stated certainty match what you established. This final read should improve the article, not produce another report.

SHORT STYLE BRIEF
These instructions guide expression. They never override factual accuracy.

- Treat the reader as an adult making a decision. Be direct, attentive and willing to make a supported recommendation. Warmth comes from noticing what matters; do not sell the destination.
- Use concrete details and explain why they affect the reader’s choice.
- Avoid promotional travel language, clichés, strained metaphors and empty praise.
- State the point directly. Avoid rhetorical formulas such as “not just X, but Y” and closers that imply a payoff without explaining it.
- Let sentence lengths vary naturally. Avoid repeated paragraph shapes, clipped sign-offs and summaries that repeat what the reader just read.
- No em dashes. Use ordinary grammar; keep useful hyphenated words.
- Cut empty hedging and commentary about your writing. Preserve dates, uncertainty and qualifications that change what the reader should do.
- Never imply you visited, ate, rode or spoke to anyone unless supplied material establishes that experience.

Do not append the existing full anti-AI block, SEO-safe block, form rulebook, work-order restrictions or repair lock afterward. That would quietly recreate the old assignment. This short style block should have one canonical owner; reconcile the existing voice/conventions files through an explicit ADR amendment, rather than maintaining competing copies.

10 / Phase two: a careful editor, not another factory

The editor reads broadly and edits narrowly. It receives the approved brief, full saved draft and research note. It can inspect relevant sources when a finding requires that. It should not manufacture a dossier, score every sentence, or buy repeated rewrites until an arbitrary score passes.

Review contract

  • Find contradictions inside the article and between the article and research note: counts, dates, price exceptions, operating status, certainty and headline promises.
  • Check whether the reader can make the promised decision and whether the article fulfills the brief without forcing unsupported detail.
  • Identify weak reasoning, repetition, unnecessary detail and prose that obscures the useful point. Do not confuse personal taste with an objective defect.
  • For every finding, quote the exact passage, explain its consequence, identify its type and say whether a source check is needed. A source-dependent finding remains unresolved until checked.
  • Prioritize a short actionable list. Do not fill a quota. A clean read can return no findings. Missing information does not license inventing a replacement fact.

Propose and apply

The initial editor should return proposed localized replacements tied to the draft version and a unique passage/section identifier. Show the before/after and a short reason. The owner can accept individual changes or all reviewed changes. Untouched text must remain unchanged. If the article needs a new structure or fresh reporting, say so as a larger proposal rather than silently regenerating it.

After accepted edits, verify the changed passages and related statements: a fare correction may affect the total and the research note; a selection change may affect the headline and closing route. Synchronize source notes when new evidence changes the article. Do not repeat a full independent research program for an edit that deletes an unnecessary sentence.

Example from preferred draftAppropriate editorial action
“Four … were still running” while body lists sevenClarify selected itinerary versus full operational count. Usually an internal-consistency edit.
Concepción tourist fare asserted while note says unresolvedCheck the cited tariff or narrow the assertion to what it supports; update body and note together.
Museum described as “your seat”Remove the unsupported leap. A researched museum is not proof of available seating.
Large restoration budget/contract paragraphCompress to what helps explain the available network. An editorial cut should not require another search.
Walking times, directions and all-four staircases assertionVerify relevant sources or omit unsupported precision. Never turn a plausible map inference into a confirmed claim.

Suggested editor prompt skeleton

Read this article as a careful editor. Preserve what already works.

Use the approved brief to understand the intended reader and purpose. Compare the article with its research note. Identify contradictions, unsupported certainty, misleading headlines, missing practical connections, repetition and unnecessary detail.

For each material finding, quote the passage, explain the reader impact, and propose the smallest adequate change. Distinguish a visible contradiction from a fact that needs source verification and from a stylistic preference. Verify source-dependent corrections before supplying a factual replacement. Never invent the missing answer.

Do not rewrite the whole article by default. Keep strong phrasing and structure. If a larger structural change is necessary, describe it separately for approval. Return no findings if no material issue is found.

Inputs: approved brief; draft version and article; research note and source links.
Output: prioritized findings, proposed localized changes, source checks performed, unresolved questions.

Editor model choice, exact UI and automated-versus-manual application beyond this initial review workflow remain phase-two decisions. The owner has approved the concept, not an unlimited autonomous rewriting loop. Model judgment is still fallible; a successful review is evidence of an inspected draft, not an accuracy guarantee.

# Valparaiso built thirty hill elevators to get people home, most of the network is dead, and the handful still running are the best two hundred pesos you will spend in the city.

Valparaíso built its hill elevators as transit for the people living up the slopes. Most of that network no longer runs. What survives is enough for one afternoon on foot.

Seven of Valparaíso's roughly thirty ascensores were confirmed running in June 2026. A foreign visitor pays 1,000 pesos a trip; a resident pays 200. Of the seven, El Peral, Concepción, Reina Victoria and Espíritu Santo put you somewhere at the top worth the climb in a single afternoon.

## What a ride costs

The fare for foreign tourists is 1,000 pesos per trip, set on 2 January 2026. Residents pay a general fare of 200 pesos over the same period, while children under 14, students with valid accreditation and anyone over 60 ride free. Ascensor Concepción is the exception: its resident fare is 300 pesos rather than 200, though a foreign visitor still pays 1,000 there. Ascensor Barón charges 600 pesos to carry a bicycle, as of 28 February 2026. You buy each trip at the station window, so four rides come to 4,000 pesos for a visitor. A visitor pays five times the general resident fare. The split is deliberate, and it is part of what the ascensores now are.

## Which ascensores are running

As of June 2026, seven were operational: Barón, Concepción, Cordillera, El Peral, Reina Victoria, Espíritu Santo and San Agustín. That fits the broader tally from October 2025, when 7 of the 16 protected historical elevators were in service. Most run Monday to Friday from 07:00 to 21:30, and Saturday, Sunday and public holidays from 08:00 to 21:30. Concepción keeps nearly the same hours, opening at 07:00 on weekdays and 08:00 at weekends and on public holidays, and closing at 21:30 throughout. The working list shifts without warning. Check each one is moving before you build a route around it.

## El Peral, up to Cerro Alegre

Start at El Peral. Its upper station opens onto Paseo Yugoslavo, a promenade on Cerro Alegre with wide views over the bay and the mountains standing behind the city. The Museo Municipal de Bellas Artes de Valparaíso sits on the promenade itself. It is the natural place to begin, because one ride puts you on the promenade with the museum at hand and the ridge walk toward Concepción starting from the same spot. A visitor pays 1,000 pesos. Weekday hours are 07:00 to 21:30, with a later 08:00 start at weekends and on holidays.

## Concepción and Reina Victoria, along the ridge

From Paseo Yugoslavo, follow the ridge to Cerro Concepción and Paseo Gervasoni, the top of Ascensor Concepción. Concepción opened in 1883 and is the oldest ascensor in the city, and it was confirmed running in May and June 2026. Paseo Gervasoni looks out over the port, and the streets around it are lined with painted houses, cafés and restaurants. A visitor pays 1,000 pesos here, the resident fare 300.

Then Reina Victoria. Its upper station sits on Pasaje Dimalow, and the exit opens into Plaza Lukas, a small square whose murals show 120 characters from the Bestiario del Reyno de Chile. From there you can ride down toward the lower town. A visitor pays 1,000 pesos, on the standard 07:00 to 21:30 weekday schedule.

## Espíritu Santo, to the open-air museum

Espíritu Santo climbs to Cerro Bellavista. Its upper station opens straight into the Museo a Cielo Abierto, a set of murals across the hill's walls and staircases, and La Sebastiana, one of Pablo Neruda's houses, stands close by. A visitor pays 1,000 pesos, on weekday hours of 07:00 to 21:30 and 08:00 to 21:30 at weekends and on holidays.

## Barón, and the two you can skip

Keep Barón in reserve. Its upper station is on Avenida Diego Portales on Cerro Barón, further east than the rest, and it gives onto the port and the bay. Barón was the first electric ascensor in Valparaíso, working since 1909. A visitor pays 1,000 pesos, a resident 200, a bicycle 600.

Cordillera and San Agustín were also running in June 2026. Cordillera's upper station is on Calle Canal, near the Museo Lord Cochrane. San Agustín opens onto a neighbourhood passage with little on it for a first visit. Both keep the standard hours. Neither is worth changing the route for.

## The rest of the network

About thirty ascensores were built from the 1880s onward, though the figure runs from 26 to 31 depending on whether short-lived, rebuilt and private lines are counted. Sixteen still stand. All are protected as national monuments. In May and June 2026 these were not running: Arrayán, Artillería, Florida, Hospital Van Buren, La Cruz, Larraín, Las Cañas, Los Lecheros, Mariposas, Monjas, Polanco, Santo Domingo and Villaseca. Polanco, the only true vertical elevator among them, went out of service on 5 September 2024 and is in the design stage for its recovery.

## Takeaways

For half an afternoon, ride four. El Peral first, up to Paseo Yugoslavo on Cerro Alegre, then the ridge to Concepción and Paseo Gervasoni. Carry on to Reina Victoria and Plaza Lukas, then Espíritu Santo onto Cerro Bellavista and the murals of the open-air museum below La Sebastiana. Every trip is 1,000 pesos at the window for a visitor, 200 for a resident, 300 at Concepción. Hold Barón back for its view east over the port if the light lasts. Confirm each is running on the day, because the list changes.

# Four of Valparaíso's hill lifts were still running this year. Here is what they cost and where they leave you.

Start with the number you will hand over. Since 2 January 2026 the municipal tariff has been 200 pesos for the general fare, 300 pesos on the Concepción, 600 pesos to take a bicycle up the Barón or the Cordillera, and 1,000 pesos for foreign tourists. Children under 14, students with a valid credential and people over 60 ride free. So a resident going home from work pays 200 pesos and you pay 1,000, about five times as much, for the same thirty to sixty seconds of cable. That gap is deliberate and the city is open about why: the ascensores cost more to run than they earn, and the visitor fare is part of what keeps them turning. If you are Chilean and down from Santiago for the day, you pay the general fare, not the tourist one.

The network runs on a winter timetable of roughly 07:00 to 21:30 Monday to Friday, and 08:00 to 21:30 on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays. Individual stations have posted slightly different closing times in the past, with the Espíritu Santo listed at 22:00. Seven public ascensores were confirmed operating on 22 May 2026: Barón, Concepción, Cordillera, El Peral, Reina Victoria, Espíritu Santo and San Agustín. The same seven were listed as operating in a guide published on 22 June 2026. Nothing more recent than that has been verified for this article, and these machines break down without warning, so treat the four below as very likely running rather than certain. The stations are open-fronted. You can see whether a car is moving before you pay.

Four of them, west to east, make a walkable half afternoon.

## Cordillera, from Serrano into the Barrio Puerto

Lower station at Serrano 230, a few minutes uphill from Plaza Sotomayor and the Puerto metro station. You pay 1,000 pesos as a foreign visitor, 200 as a resident. Winter hours as above. The approach is the nicest of the four: a small plaza before the door with a pergola, a lamp and somewhere to sit while you wait. The car climbs about sixty metres of track on a gradient near 70 degrees, the steepest in the city, and takes about thirty seconds. You step out at the Plazuela Eleuterio Ramírez, known locally as the Plaza Merlet, where Calle Castillo runs on up the hill. This is a residential cerro, not a viewpoint strip. The Museo Lord Cochrane sits alongside on Calle Merlet, on ground with a terrace looking down over Plaza Sotomayor and the working port, though its future use was under official review and I could not confirm it is open. Seating on the plazuela itself I could not confirm; the reliable bench is the one at the bottom.

## El Peral, from the courthouse to Paseo Yugoslavo

Lower station on Plaza de la Justicia, beside the courts building, eight minutes east of Plaza Sotomayor. Foreign visitor 1,000 pesos, resident 200. The upper station opens straight onto Paseo Yugoslavo, a terrace built in 1915 directly above Plaza Sotomayor, where you look down on cranes, tugs and naval vessels working the country's main port. People here lean on the railings rather than sit; I found no confirmed benches. What there is instead is the Palacio Baburizza across the paseo, home to the Museo Municipal de Bellas Artes, published hours Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00 from April through December. That is your seat, and a good one.

## Concepción, the oldest, up to Paseo Gervasoni

Lower station in the Elías passage off Calle Prat, facing the Reloj Turri, five minutes on foot from Paseo Yugoslavo along the hilltop if you would rather walk down and ride up. Opened 1883. This is the one with the different resident fare: 300 pesos rather than 200, and 1,000 for foreign visitors. The upper station puts you on Paseo Gervasoni on Cerro Concepción, a cobbled lookout with wrought-iron railings, artisans selling work along it, the Casa Mirador de Lukas and Café Turri. It has benches, which makes it the obvious place to stop if you have been climbing stairs all afternoon.

## Reina Victoria, up to Paseo Dimalow

Lower station on Elías, reached from Subida Cumming off Plaza Aníbal Pinto. Note that both this and the Concepción sit on an alley called Elías, at opposite ends of the Prat and Condell run, which catches people out. Foreign visitor 1,000 pesos, resident 200. Short and steep. The upper station opens onto a small arrival square with seats, a playground and lighting, connected by a bridge to Paseo Dimalow. From that square you look across at Cerro Panteón and Cerro Cárcel; from Dimalow itself you get the murals, the wooden walkways and the bay.

The rest of the network is honestly dead. Around thirty ascensores operated from 1883; sixteen structures survive, and only seven of those carry the public. The Van Buren still runs but serves the hospital and is not open to you. The Polanco, the only true vertical lift, has been out of service and in restoration and design work through 2026. The Artillería, the postcard one above the port, has been stopped for more than five years; a Ministry of Public Works tender to restore its two stations, budgeted at just over 6,132 million pesos, was due to be awarded on 31 August 2026, with no announced reopening date. Work on the Monjas and Villaseca halted at 60 and 62 per cent when a contract ended early. Funding approvals for the Florida and the Mariposas expired without a design. The Larraín waits on an easement over private land.

Every one of the four has a public staircase running alongside the rails. Ride up, walk down, and you have spent 4,000 pesos on the afternoon.
