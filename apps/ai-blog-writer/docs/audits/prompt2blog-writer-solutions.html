Seven problems.
Seven concrete fixes.

A practical solution plan for the writer audit, with examples, implementation boundaries, and acceptance checks.

Based on the writer audit in this task · Recommendations, not implemented changes

SolutionsBuild orderValidation

Fix reliability first. These seven changes address the failure mechanisms already discussed. They preserve the v4 brief, frozen selection, single voice, and advisory status after writing.

01 / Seven fixes

What to change, and how to prove it

01High

Let grounding see your personal notes

Problem: The writer sees first-hand material in the brief; the checker may not. Valid experience can look invented.

Solution: Give grounding the same verbatim personal material the writer received, clearly separated from researched claims.

“I waited 45 minutes on my visit” stays supported as personal testimony. “Everyone waits 45 minutes” remains unsupported.

Implementation: Freeze material IDs, exact statements, and kind alongside the writing packet. Grounding reads the full dossier plus this frozen material. Preserve qualifications and distinguish personal experience from general claims. This does not send first-hand material back through web research.

Proof it works: Fixture with a brief-only personal observation reaches the checker unchanged. A paraphrase preserving scope is allowed; a generalization is flagged. Conflicts between testimony and research are visible internally, not silently resolved.

Cost and tradeoff: Small-to-medium change. A little more checker context; no additional model call. Treat testimony as supplied experience, not independently verified fact.

02High

Never turn a broken check into a pass

Problem: An empty JSON object or missing findings can become checked and grounded.

Solution: Validate the response before using it. Record supported, unsupported, or unchecked as distinct outcomes.

An empty response becomes “Grounding could not be completed,” while the existing draft remains available to save.

Implementation: Require correctly typed verdict, assessment, and findings; restrict severity to known values. Use local validation even when a provider supports schemas. Reject contradictory verdicts and findings instead of silently normalizing them. Permit one bounded retry for malformed output, within the existing run budget; otherwise preserve unchecked status.

Proof it works: Test empty objects, missing fields, invalid severity, a false verdict with no explanation, and provider failure. None can earn a verified stamp. A valid clean result still passes. Final readiness is advisory and an unchecked draft stays savable.

Cost and tradeoff: Small-to-medium change. No new call on valid responses; malformed responses may cost one retry. Never confuse the retry limit with proof the article is correct.

03High

Allow repair to recover omitted selected facts

Problem: Repair sees an incomplete draft but cannot use facts the first writer omitted.

Solution: Give repair the frozen writing packet and permission to use its selected facts, while keeping unselected research out.

If the packet says one restaurant is cheaper and another has vegetarian options, repair can add that useful comparison without inventing it.

Implementation: Provide selected claim text, dates, confidence, caveats, and first-hand material. Require internal claim IDs for newly added factual material. Validate IDs against the frozen packet and re-ground the resulting draft. An ID alone does not prove the new sentence means the same thing.

Proof it works: A requested comparison can use an omitted selected fact. An unselected fact cannot be requested as an addition. Changes preserve caveats and pass a semantic grounding check. Unavailable material becomes an internal research need.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium change. More repair input, but only when repair runs. More factual freedom makes the grounding re-check essential.

04High

Make the writing rules agree

Problem: Voice, house rules, and compose instructions can ask for incompatible behavior.

Solution: Use one explicit authority order and remove obsolete or contradictory prose from active instructions.

Replace “never hedge” with “avoid empty hedging; keep uncertainty that changes the meaning.” Keep research gaps in internal notes.

Implementation: Keep one Questurian Voice file and the short mechanical conventions file. Inventory each active rule, assign one owner, and remove duplicate legacy commission language. Evidence and its qualifications constrain factual claims; the approved brief controls intent; form controls structure; voice controls expression. Review shared-file consumers before changing shared rules.

Proof it works: Inspect actual assembled prompts for representative forms. No active instruction asks both to hide and display gaps, or both to preserve and erase uncertainty. Human review checks meaning; string checks alone cannot establish consistency.

Cost and tradeoff: Small-to-medium change. No extra model calls; likely less prompt text. Rule removal must retain useful accuracy constraints.

05Medium

Keep style cleanup from changing facts

Problem: The cleanup pass sees the article and style errors without the original evidence context.

Solution: Prefer style detection plus targeted edits. Avoid an unrestricted full-article rewrite for a local style issue.

An edit can replace a cliché without turning “offered in March” into “offers today.”

Implementation: Retain cheap deterministic checks. Use deterministic edits only where meaning is unaffected. For semantic edits, use the section-edit mechanism from solution 6, with the brief and relevant frozen facts and caveats. Re-ground the changed draft. Update the ADR and enforcement tests together when replacing the current pass.

Proof it works: Dates, currencies, negations, qualifiers, and quoted experience survive style corrections. A cleanup cannot touch an unrelated section. Unresolved style issues remain advisory rather than triggering repeated rewrites.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium change. Smaller edit requests may save tokens, but measure total cost. Overly mechanical replacements can also damage meaning.

06Medium

Replace only the sections that need repair

Problem: A full-article repair can change good prose even when instructed not to.

Solution: Return structured section replacements and apply them in code. Preserve every other section exactly.

A pricing repair replaces the pricing section. The opening and recommendation remain byte-for-byte unchanged.

Implementation: Assign stable IDs to sections, including opening and closing blocks. Send approved target IDs, original text hashes, revisions, and supporting material. Reject unknown IDs, duplicates, or stale hashes. Apply valid replacements to the original document. Structural edits that require multiple sections must name them explicitly; re-audit the complete assembled article.

Proof it works: Untargeted sections compare exactly before and after. Duplicate headings cannot confuse IDs. Stale edits are rejected. A failed replacement preserves the existing draft; it does not make saving impossible.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium-to-large change. Requires reliable Markdown segmentation and version checks. Local edits can still break transitions, so whole-article review remains necessary.

07Medium

Let the article form choose its structure

Problem: Every article gets a direct-answer opening, several headings, and takeaways, regardless of form.

Solution: Resolve structure from the approved form, using the same rules in outline, compose, and deterministic checks.

A service guide can open with a direct answer. A chef profile can open with a supported detail and develop a portrait. Neither is forced into the other’s structure.

Implementation: Add explicit form policies for opening, heading expectations, and closing. Remove universal requirements from compose where the form should decide. Keep evidence discipline and the single voice shared. Record the resolved policy in the frozen runtime so resume uses the same rules.

Proof it works: A service guide retains useful answer structure. A profile is not penalized for lacking takeaways. An interview can preserve Q&A structure. A form never licenses invented scenes or quotations.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium change. No extra model call. Flexible structure needs a small set of clear defaults, not another form questionnaire for users.

02 / Build order

Deliver in four groups

  1. Grounding integrity — solutions 2 and 1

    Validate checker responses, then align the writer’s and checker’s supplied material.

  2. One writing contract — solutions 4 and 7

    Remove contradictory rules and let each form resolve its own structure.

  3. Safe repair — solutions 6 and 3

    Establish section replacements, then let those replacements use the frozen selected evidence.

  4. Safer style cleanup — solution 5

    Reuse targeted editing instead of building a second rewrite mechanism.

03 / Acceptance

Prove mechanics and prose separately

Use deterministic fixtures for malformed checks, material handoff, evidence IDs, frozen inputs, and unchanged sections. Then compare drafts from the same frozen packets across several forms, including first-hand material, dated facts, conflicting evidence, and sparse research.

Have a person compare reader usefulness, voice, preserved caveats, and editing effort without seeing which version produced the draft. Record calls, tokens, latency, and cost. Keep existing drafts available throughout; no new post-writing approval gate.
