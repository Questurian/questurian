A better writer.
Beyond the fixes.

Eight feature proposals to improve reader usefulness, editorial control, continuity, and measurable writing quality.

Based on the writer audit in this task · Recommendations, not implemented changes

FeaturesRoadmapBoundariesSuccess measures

Improve the article, not the number of AI passes. Build on the current brief, packet, outline, and audit. These are proposed features and experiments—not claims that changing a model will automatically improve the prose.

01 / Eight additions

Features with a clear editorial job

01Build first

A reader decision for each section

Make every section earn its place.

For a neighborhood comparison, a section should answer “Which suits an early flight?” rather than merely describe both neighborhoods.

How it works: Extend the existing outline with one reader purpose per section: a decision for service content, an insight for a profile, or a question answered for an interview. Let the existing audit check delivery and cite a specific missing payoff. Do not turn every form into advice.

Cost and tradeoff: Mostly prompt and schema work; no additional stage required. Can become formulaic if every section is forced to issue a recommendation.

Success looks like: Human readers can identify what each section helped them understand or decide. Track unresolved promises, not just headings completed.

02Build next

See where every important claim came from

Make factual edits reviewable without cluttering the article.

Click a price in the editor to see its selected claim, as-of date, and caveat. The published prose stays clean.

How it works: Store an internal mapping from passage IDs to claim or first-hand material IDs. Generate or verify mappings against the final draft, invalidate them when a passage changes, and label automatic matches as provisional. Include operator material and exceptions for judgment.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium-to-large feature. Alignment may need an additional model pass; start with an optional review action. A linked ID proves provenance only when the statement’s meaning also matches.

Success looks like: Sample linked passages manually. Measure correct mappings, missing links, and lost qualifications; never use link count as proof of truth.

03Build next

A short article memory for targeted edits

Keep local edits aware of the whole piece.

A restaurant section knows that the opening already explains the price range and the conclusion recommends one option for vegetarians.

How it works: Build a compact internal summary of the draft’s thesis, named entities, decisions, terms, and section purposes. Pass it to targeted repair as navigation only. Facts and caveats still come from the frozen packet, never from this summary. Refresh it after accepted edits.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium feature. A generated summary adds cost and can drift. Start with outline plus exact excerpts before adding summarization.

Success looks like: Readers see fewer repeated explanations and fewer contradictory recommendations after edits. Test that a mistaken memory summary cannot override evidence.

04Build first

Give every useful detail room to work

Help the writer explain a fact instead of stacking facts.

A short food section can use three telling details and explain their significance instead of listing ten dishes with no judgment.

How it works: Extend existing crowded-section diagnostics rather than adding a second density system. Show planned fact load against the section budget, repeated claims, and optional selected details. Let the writer omit spare details; treat density as advice, never a hard maximum.

Cost and tradeoff: Small-to-medium feature; much can be computed from the outline and claim IDs. Numbers can be misleading because one complex fact may need more explanation than several simple facts.

Success looks like: Human review finds less catalog-like prose without losing must-name obligations or qualifications. Compare density diagnostics with actual reader judgments.

05Experiment later

Learn from edits you actually accept

Improve the existing house voice using real editorial feedback.

If you repeatedly replace vague recommendations with a clear choice and its tradeoff, record that pattern for review.

How it works: Capture accepted before/after excerpts and an optional reason. Group recurring edits, then propose a small change to the single Questurian Voice or conventions file. Human approval controls canonical rule changes; do not silently learn new instructions or introduce tone profiles.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium-to-large feature. Requires edit history and careful attribution. A correction appropriate to one article should not become a universal rule.

Success looks like: Future drafts need fewer edits for the targeted pattern, without regressing other forms. Compare on held-out examples instead of the excerpts that motivated the rule.

06Build first

A repeatable writing-quality comparison set

Know whether a prompt change made articles better.

Compare old and new output from the same restaurant packet, profile packet, and sparse service-guide packet. Hide version labels from the reviewer.

How it works: Save approved, frozen briefs and packets as evaluation fixtures. Include varied forms, first-hand details, dated facts, and evidence conflicts. Version prompts and model settings. Score usefulness, unsupported additions, caveat retention, voice, repetition, edit effort, and cost separately.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium feature. Fresh model comparisons cost money and should run deliberately, not on every small edit. Provider variation means one winning sample proves little.

Success looks like: A candidate improves human preference and editing effort on multiple cases without weakening factual support. Retain regressions and compare repeated samples before broad rollout.

07Build next

An editor-controlled rewrite with a clear diff

Let you request a specific improvement without losing good prose.

Select a paragraph, choose “make the recommendation clearer,” and inspect the proposed replacement beside the original.

How it works: Reuse section-scoped edits, frozen facts, and version hashes. Offer a few useful actions: clarify, shorten, reduce repetition, or strengthen a supported comparison. Show changes before applying; retain undo and the original draft. This is an optional editor tool, not a mandatory new gate.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium-to-large UI feature. Each request can incur a model call. User requests may be impossible within the available evidence; say so instead of filling gaps.

Success looks like: Only selected scope changes. Undo restores the previous text. Unsupported additions are identified and the user can keep the original without losing work.

08Build next

Catch freshness and evidence trouble early

Make weak input visible before confident prose is generated.

A selected menu price checked a year ago carries an obvious date. A current-price promise with only old prices returns to the existing research conversation.

How it works: Add deterministic diagnostics for missing dates on time-sensitive claims, broken source links between records, and conflicting selected statements. Integrate them with the existing pre-writing readiness path. Whether a fact is stale depends on its kind; do not set a universal expiry. New research changes the dossier and selection before a new packet is frozen.

Cost and tradeoff: Medium feature. Diagnostics can be cheap; fresh research is a separate cost. Dates alone cannot establish that a fact is false or current.

Success looks like: Dated evidence remains dated in prose. A snapshot article can use historical facts intentionally. No silent web enrichment or change to a packet after writing starts.

03 / Design boundaries

Keep the v4 foundation intact

The Article Brief remains the vision throughout the run. The selection controls the writer’s facts; the frozen packet does not silently expand. First-hand material stays verbatim in its record. There is one Questurian Voice, not selectable personalities.

Use the existing pre-writing research gate and return to the grill when the article’s promise cannot be supported. After prose exists, diagnostics stay advisory and drafts remain savable. Avoid automatic research expansion, mandatory extra approvals, and unbounded rewrite loops.

04 / Decision rule

Ship what reduces real editing work

Choose a baseline from the same frozen inputs. Compare human preference and editing effort alongside factual support, retained qualifications, latency, and cost. Decide acceptable tradeoffs before running a paid comparison. No percentage improvement is promised here.

Recommended first investment: the evaluation set plus section-purpose improvements. They make future changes easier to judge and improve the existing writing process without automatically adding another model call to every article.
