Day 01 / Miraflores

An easy Miraflores day built around lunch at Huaca Pucllana

This day stays inside Miraflores and asks little of your legs. It starts with sourdough and coffee, then takes in the Pacific from the top of the cliffs at Parque del Amor. Its weight falls on a long lunch of Peruvian classics at a restaurant set inside Huaca Pucllana, an archaeological site of the Lima culture. The afternoon trades views for the craft galleries on Avenida Petit Thouars, then leaves a long gap to rest. Dinner is deliberately small. Every move between stops is a short taxi ride, and nothing on the route involves cliff stairs or a walk down to the beach.

El Pan de la Chola (Alcanfores)

Calle Alcanfores 410, Miraflores, Lima

El Pan de la Chola is a Lima bakery that works with natural fermentation and local flours, some of them ground on its own stone mill. Its Alcanfores branch opens at 7am every day. Keep breakfast simple: a croissant or a morning bun with a cappuccino, or avocado and egg on sourdough toast if you want more. The point is to eat lightly. Lunch is the meal this day is built around, and anything heavier now works against it.

What to do & practical details
  • Order coffee with a pastry such as a classic croissant or a morning bun
  • Choose a toast, such as avocado and egg or hummus, if a pastry is not enough
  • Posted hours: Monday to Sunday, 7am–9pm.
  • As listed on the branch menu in September 2026: classic croissant PEN 8, cappuccino PEN 11, avocado and egg toast PEN 24.
  • The bakery states that allergens are present in its kitchens and keeps an allergen sheet at the counter.
Why this stop · evidence

A gentle opener in central Miraflores, open from early morning every day, with a short taxi ride to the clifftop park.

It is an official Miraflores branch of a sourdough bakery with posted daily hours starting at 7am. Its branch menu lists coffee, pastries and light toasts, which meets the 'light breakfast or coffee' and 'bakery' criteria. It was chosen over the chain's La Mar branches because Alcanfores is on the southern, clifftop side of central Miraflores, which suits the morning route.

El Pan de la Chola's Alcanfores branch is at Calle Alcanfores 410, Miraflores, and posts hours of Monday to Sunday, 7am–9pm.

02 / attractions

Parque del Amor (Love Park)

Malecón Cisneros, block 4, Miraflores, Lima

Parque del Amor sits on Malecón Cisneros at the edge of the Miraflores cliffs, so the Pacific is laid out below you without a single step down to the shore. The park opened on 14 February 1993 around El Beso, Víctor Delfín's large sculpture of a couple kissing. Its tiled surrounds carry lines by Peruvian poets including Martín Adán and César Moro, and the design draws on Gaudí's Park Güell. An hour is enough: look at the sea, read the walls, then cross to Marcelo Wong's Cupido sculpture opposite.

What to do & practical details
  • Take in the Pacific view from the clifftop
  • Look at El Beso and read the poets' lines set into the tiled surrounds
  • Cross to see Marcelo Wong's Cupido sculpture directly opposite the park
  • Entry is free and the park is open 24 hours; the recommended visiting time is 8am–9pm.
  • Public toilets and food and drink kiosks are listed at the park.
  • Ramps are listed for visitors with physical disabilities.
Why this stop · evidence

The low-effort coastal highlight: an ocean view from the top of the cliffs, reached by taxi, with no beach descent.

Official municipal and MINCETUR records place the park at the edge of the Malecón Cisneros cliffs, with a view of the Pacific, free entry and 24-hour access. This meets the 'coastal view from above' and 'no beach descent' requirements. The park also has substance beyond the view (the Delfín sculpture and the poetry mosaics). Larcomar was rejected because it is a shopping centre and would overlap with the afternoon shopping stop.

Parque del Amor is on Malecón Cisneros (block 4), Miraflores. It was inaugurated on 14 February 1993 and centres on the sculpture El Beso by Víctor Delfín. Its surrounds are decorated with verses by Peruvian poets including Martín Adán and César Moro.

03 / dining

Restaurante Huaca Pucllana

Calle General Borgoño, block 8, Huaca Pucllana, Miraflores, Lima

Restaurante Huaca Pucllana is set inside Huaca Pucllana, a site of the Lima culture dating to between 200 and 700 AD, and you eat with a view of the ruins. The menu is à la carte, so you can linger over a long lunch without committing to a tasting menu. Order from the section headed Tradición peruana, which includes chupe de camarones, cau cau, lomo saltado and seco de lomo, or start with a classic ceviche. The restaurant says 7.6% of its sales go toward restoring the monument.

What to do & practical details
  • Book a table ahead through the restaurant's official booking link
  • Order from the Tradición peruana section, or begin with the classic ceviche
  • Take your time; nothing is scheduled until mid-afternoon
  • Posted hours: Monday to Saturday 12pm–10pm, Sunday 12pm–5pm.
  • Bookings are taken through the link on the restaurant's website, or by email at reservas@resthuacapucllana.com.
  • On the menu posted in September 2026, the Tradición peruana dishes cost PEN 51–74 and the classic ceviche PEN 55. Prices include taxes and service.
Why this stop · evidence

The food anchor of the day: an à la carte Peruvian menu in a setting inside an archaeological site, all within Miraflores.

The official site identifies this as a Peruvian restaurant inside Huaca Pucllana in Miraflores. The official menu is à la carte with a dedicated Peruvian-tradition section, and El Comercio reports its view over the Lima-culture archaeological site. This meets 'distinctly Peruvian', 'a la carte' and 'special setting' without leaving Miraflores or using Barranco. Its posted hours cover a noon lunch on every day of the week.

Boulevard Artesanal de Miraflores (Avenida Petit Thouars craft galleries)

Av. Petit Thouars, blocks 52–55, Miraflores; start at Mercado Indio, Av. Petit Thouars 5245

After a view and a long lunch, the afternoon turns to shopping. Blocks 52 to 55 of Avenida Petit Thouars hold 14 craft galleries with more than 1,500 artisans between them. Their work comes from across Peru: woodcarving, textiles, ceramics, carved gourds (mates burilados), silverwork and retablos. This is shopping aimed at visitors rather than a neighbourhood market, and it is easy to cut short. Walk one side, cross, walk back, and leave when you have had enough. Indian Market and Inka Plaza are both on the 5300 block.

What to do & practical details
  • Browse the galleries between Mercado Indio at number 5245 and the 5400 block
  • Look for regional work such as mates burilados, retablos and silverwork
  • Stop when you have had enough; nothing later depends on this stop
  • Mercado Indio, at number 5245, posts opening hours of every day until 8pm.
Why this stop · evidence

It contrasts with the morning: streets and shops instead of a view, and it is easy to cut short before the rest break.

The Miraflores municipal tourism site lists this handicraft boulevard, its location on blocks 52–55 and the range of crafts sold. It adds shops and street-level texture and does not repeat the morning's scenic park. It can be shortened at will. Mercado Indio's posted daily hours cover the afternoon slot.

The Miraflores handicraft boulevard occupies blocks 52–55 of Av. Petit Thouars and houses more than 1,500 artisans in 14 galleries. Its crafts from across the country include woodcarving, textiles, ceramics, mates burilados, jewellery, silverwork and retablos.

05 / dining

Pardos Chicken (Benavides)

Av. Benavides 730, Miraflores, Lima

Dinner is small on purpose. Pardos Chicken is a Peruvian chain built around pollo a la brasa. Its branch on Avenida Benavides opens every day from noon until at least 11pm, so an early table is no problem. After the lunch at Huaca Pucllana, order from the sandwich or salad sections, or share the chicken between two. An hour is plenty. This is the meal that lets you eat well today and still have energy for tomorrow.

What to do & practical details
  • Order from the sandwich or salad sections, or share a chicken order
  • Keep dinner to about an hour
  • Posted hours: Monday to Thursday 12pm–12am, Friday and Saturday 12pm–1am, Sunday 12pm–11pm.
  • The branch takes reservations by phone (241-0215 or 998 158 770) and by email (reservas@pardosbenavides.com).
Why this stop · evidence

A casual, lighter recovery meal in central Miraflores after the main lunch, open early enough for an 18:30 table every day.

The official branch page confirms the Miraflores address and daily hours that cover an 18:30 dinner. The chain's official menu includes sandwich and salad sections, so the reader can keep the meal lighter than lunch while still eating distinctly Peruvian food. La Lucha Sanguchería was considered as a lighter sandwich option but rejected because its official site shows no current hours.

06 / nightlife · Optional

Haití (café, bar and restaurant)

Av. Diagonal 160, Miraflores, Lima

Haití has faced the Parque Central de Miraflores since 23 February 1962. It is a café, bar and restaurant that grew out of a café opened on Plaza Pizarro in 1952. It stays open until 1am every day, so you are nowhere near closing time: take a table and order an espresso or a glass from the wine list. Skip it if the day has already been enough. Nothing later depends on it.

What to do & practical details
  • Order an espresso or a drink from the wine list
  • Leave by about 20:30 to reach your hotel by 21:00
  • Posted hours: Monday to Sunday, 7:30am–1am.
Why this stop · evidence

An optional, conversation-friendly café-bar finish near dinner in central Miraflores, open well past the day's end.

The official site describes Haití as a family-run restaurant, café and bar with a long history on the Parque Central de Miraflores, a wine list and daily hours to 1am. That suits a quiet, social finish that is not a club. It stays optional; removing it leaves the required day intact.

Haití is at Av. Diagonal 160, Miraflores, and posts hours of Monday to Sunday, 07:30am–01:00am.
